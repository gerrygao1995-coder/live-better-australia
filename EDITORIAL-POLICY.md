# Editorial policy

This guide helps readers find a sensible next action. It is a broad educational resource for people living in Australia, including citizens and residents with different eligibility conditions, locations, abilities, incomes and family situations.

## What the labels mean

**Official rule** identifies a requirement, entitlement or process explained by the authority responsible for it. A summary can leave out exceptions; the linked official source and the reader's circumstances remain decisive.

**Official / professional guidance** identifies guidance from a government agency, regulator, recognised professional organisation or relevant primary service provider. It does not mean that every sentence is a research result, that the evidence is equally strong across topics, or that an organisation endorses this guide.

**Practical suggestion** identifies editorial judgement: a planning method, question to ask or organisational habit. These entries do not claim measured benefits or a universal best choice. A source may be linked for context without converting the whole suggestion into an official recommendation.

The action is the central instruction. **Cost / effort** and **Potential value** are qualitative editorial assessments, not quotations, measured savings or promised outcomes. **Applies to** is a scope cue, not an eligibility determination.

## Review dates and change

This first edition was prepared and editorially reviewed on **9 October 2026**. Linked sources were consulted during preparation; review used readable official pages, documents and, where direct access was limited, indexed primary-source text. A date does not certify every linked page's ongoing availability or every possible exception. Practical entries carry an editorial review date, not an evidence-verification date.

Check the live source before acting on a deadline, benefit, price, legal process, medical recommendation or eligibility rule. Program names, terms, provider fees and state legislation can change. Federal guidance does not automatically settle a state or territory question. A link that loads successfully does not, by itself, validate a claim.

The repository does not run an automatic policy-monitoring service. Future editions should prioritise urgent errors, changed rules and broken primary links, then improve coverage and clarity. Substantial corrections belong in the changelog.

## Health, law and money

Individual circumstances matter. The guide does not diagnose conditions, select treatment, assess a legal case, determine entitlement, calculate a person's tax liability or recommend an investment product. Use it to prepare questions and find the appropriate professional or official pathway. In immediate danger call **000**.

Avoid turning a broad recommendation into a universal screening age, medication dose, rebate promise or legal deadline. When a precise rule is necessary, identify the jurisdiction, effective date, scope and authoritative source.

## Source and writing standards

- Prefer the agency administering the rule, a regulator, a recognised professional body or the service provider responsible for the pathway.
- Link to the page supporting the claim. Use a landing page only when the next step is intentionally to find local details there.
- Use Australian English and concrete actions. Explain acronyms when they may be unfamiliar.
- Avoid numerical savings, health-effect claims, comparative rankings and guarantees without suitable evidence and context.
- Keep lived experience and practical advice welcome, while labelling them honestly.
- Do not add affiliate links, sponsored rankings, referral codes or invented testimonials.
- Preserve upstream attribution and third-party rights. Rephrase accurately and quote sparingly.

## Preparation and limitations

This edition was prepared with AI assistance, source consultation, editorial checks and automated structural validation. It has not been independently reviewed by medical, legal or financial professionals. The number of entries measures coverage, not scientific quality or the number of distinct research findings.

The project adapts the original guide at the level of themes. It does not reproduce every original recommendation. Readers can inspect the [source map](SOURCE-MAP.md), [source directory](SOURCES.md) and [attribution](ATTRIBUTION.md), and propose corrections through the repository.
