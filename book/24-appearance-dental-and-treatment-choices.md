## AU-0415 · Start acne care with a manageable routine

Ask a pharmacist or GP which basic skin care and treatment suit your acne and other medicines. Use products as directed and avoid squeezing lesions or repeatedly adding harsh products. Introduce changes deliberately so irritation and treatment effects are easier to discuss at review.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Basic products cost A$; avoid buying several overlapping treatments. |
| Potential value | A simpler routine that is easier to follow and assess. |
| Applies to | People with acne, including teenagers and adults. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Healthdirect: acne](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/acne)

## AU-0416 · Seek help when acne scars or affects wellbeing

Book a GP appointment if acne is painful, scarring, persistent or affecting confidence and daily life. Bring the names of products you have tried and how you used them. Ask when to review treatment and whether specialist care is appropriate, rather than assuming cosmetic products can solve every form of acne.

| Potential value | Earlier discussion of options for more troublesome acne. |
| Applies to | People whose acne is not manageable with basic care. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0417 · Ask for an eczema treatment plan

Discuss moisturising, trigger management and prescribed flare treatment with a clinician or pharmacist. Ask which product goes where, how it should be used and what changes require review. Avoid substituting an expensive 'natural' product for an agreed treatment without checking ingredients and suitability.

## AU-0418 · Treat an itchy or flaky scalp as a health concern

Ask a pharmacist about suitable dandruff treatment and follow the product instructions. Arrange medical advice if symptoms are persistent, severe, painful or accompanied by hair loss or other skin changes. A changing scalp problem may need assessment rather than repeated purchases of cosmetic shampoo.

## AU-0419 · Assess hair loss before buying a long package

Discuss new, patchy or distressing hair loss with a GP, including timing, scalp symptoms, medicines and recent health changes. Ask what type of hair loss is suspected and which options have evidence for it. Clarify ongoing costs, side effects and the likely review period before committing to treatment.

## AU-0420 · Check oral health before whitening teeth

Ask a dentist whether whitening is appropriate for your teeth, gums and existing dental work. Discuss sensitivity, expected results and alternatives before buying treatment. Whitening does not address decay or gum disease, and restorations may not change colour in the same way as natural teeth.

| Applies to | Adults considering cosmetic teeth whitening. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0421 · Get the complete orthodontic proposal

Ask what problem treatment is intended to address, the available options and what happens if you wait. Request the total anticipated fee, appointments, likely treatment period and follow-up arrangements in writing. ## AU-0422 · Do not ignore recurring bleeding gums

Arrange dental advice for persistent bleeding, swelling, bad breath or loose teeth. Continue gentle cleaning as advised rather than stopping all cleaning because the gums bleed. Ask the dental team to demonstrate a technique and cleaning tools suited to your mouth.

## AU-0423 · Review persistent dry mouth

Tell a dentist or doctor about ongoing dryness and bring a list of medicines and supplements. Ask about the cause, symptom relief and additional protection for your teeth. Do not stop or reduce a medicine yourself, and avoid relying on frequent sugary drinks or sweets for relief.

## AU-0424 · Know what to do after a tooth is knocked out

Contact a dentist urgently after dental trauma. Never put a knocked-out baby tooth back; for a permanent tooth, seek immediate advice and keep it moist in milk if it cannot be safely replaced. Handle it by the visible crown, avoid scrubbing the root and seek emergency help for serious associated injury.

| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Australian Dental Association: Dental trauma](https://teeth.org.au/dental-trauma)

## AU-0425 · Check cosmetic practitioners before paying

Verify a claimed doctor or nurse's registration and any conditions on the Ahpra register, then ask about qualifications and experience for the specific procedure. ## AU-0426 · Budget for the full surgical recovery

Request an itemised cosmetic-surgery quote covering the surgeon, anaesthesia, facility, follow-up and possible additional treatment. Add time off work, travel and practical help during recovery to your own budget. Confirm any claimed Medicare or insurance contribution directly; cosmetic treatment is generally not covered.

## AU-0427 · Check whether a cosmetic goal is realistic

Explain the change you want and ask the clinician what the procedure can and cannot achieve, including scars, complications and further treatment. Take time to consider your reasons and other options. If appearance worries dominate everyday life, discuss that distress with a GP before treating surgery as the solution.

## AU-0428 · Treat injectables as a medical procedure

Ask who will assess you, prescribe where required and perform the injections, and verify their registration and relevant experience. Request the product name, risks, aftercare and emergency contact details before consenting. A low price or a social-media recommendation does not establish that a product or practitioner is suitable.

## AU-0429 · Get a changing skin spot checked

Know what is usual for your skin and book medical assessment for a new or changing spot or a sore that does not heal. Ask your doctor about a skin-check schedule suited to your risk. Do not rely on a phone app or wait for a routine screening appointment to assess a concerning change.

## AU-0430 · Check hygiene before a tattoo

Ask how the business handles sterile equipment, single-use items, ink and written aftercare. Check the relevant local registration and age or consent requirements; NSW rules cited here are not a national licence system. Avoid tattooing infected or damaged skin and seek medical advice for suspected infection afterwards.

## AU-0431 · Check public dental assistance early

Use your state or territory health service to check public dental eligibility, fees and waiting arrangements. For a child, separately check Child Dental Benefits Schedule eligibility and the proposed treatment's coverage. Tell the service about pain or swelling so it can assess urgency rather than assuming a routine waiting list is the only option.

## AU-0432 · Ask why each proposed dental procedure is needed

Request an explanation of the problem, treatment options, likely benefits and risks of waiting. Get item numbers and a written quote, then check any insurance benefit directly. For a substantial non-urgent treatment plan, a second opinion can help clarify alternatives and which work needs priority.

| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | A$0 for clarification; another assessment may have a fee. |
| Potential value | A clearer treatment decision and more accurate cost estimate. |
| Applies to | People offered significant dental treatment. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |
