# 34. Buying wisely and avoiding traps

Good value depends on the job a purchase must do, its full cost and whether you will use it. These are practical decision checks, not promises that one brand or price point always wins.

## AU-0595 · Define the job before comparing products

Write the few tasks the purchase must perform and the conditions in which you will use it. Separate essential requirements from attractive extras, then compare products against the same list. If an item solves a problem you do not actually have, a discount does not make it the right choice for your household.

## AU-0596 · Compare like-for-like unit prices

When pack sizes differ, calculate the price per usable unit using the same measure and check the product itself is comparable. Include how much you are likely to use before it spoils or becomes unwanted, rather than automatically choosing the largest pack. Keep convenience and storage constraints visible alongside the arithmetic.

## AU-0597 · Estimate the full ownership cost

For a durable purchase, list the purchase price, necessary accessories, consumables, servicing, delivery and expected running needs. Mark uncertain figures as estimates and use a realistic period of ownership rather than assuming the longest possible life. Compare affordable alternatives using the same assumptions before deciding whether a low sticker price represents good value.

## AU-0598 · Check consumables before choosing the device

Find out which cartridges, filters, bags, batteries or other supplies the product requires and whether compatible options are readily available. Compare their current prices and likely replacement pattern with your expected use, asking the seller for information you cannot verify. Avoid committing to a cheap device whose essential supplies do not fit your budget.

## AU-0599 · Ask about repair options before a major purchase

Check whether common parts, local servicing and repair information are available for the exact model. Ask what happens if it fails outside the seller's included service arrangements, without assuming a repair will always be economical. Consider ease of maintenance alongside features, especially where losing the item would disrupt work or essential household tasks.

## AU-0600 · Measure the space and the route into it

Measure where the item will be used and stored, plus doors, stairs, lifts and access needed for delivery. Compare those measurements with the exact product and packaging dimensions, including clearance for use or ventilation where specified. Confirm installation requirements before paying rather than discovering that the bargain cannot be placed safely where intended.

## AU-0601 · Test comfort and accessibility in real use

Where possible, try the controls, weight, handles, screen and ordinary tasks rather than judging from a display photograph. Include the person who will use the product and any accessibility needs, and ask about a suitable trial or return arrangement before buying. An impressive specification is only useful if the item works comfortably in practice.

## AU-0602 · Try a modest version before committing heavily

For a new hobby or unfamiliar routine, begin with a small, suitable purchase or short trial that lets you learn what you need. Notice which features matter through use before buying the whole premium kit. Set a review point so the experiment informs the next purchase instead of becoming a series of impulsive upgrades.

## AU-0603 · Sample before buying in bulk

If a product is new to you, try a small quantity before committing to a large order, especially when taste, fit or compatibility matters. Work out where it will be stored and whether you can use it within the relevant period. A lower unit price is helpful only for the amount that actually serves a purpose.

## AU-0604 · Compare borrowing, hiring and owning

Estimate how often you will use the item and compare purchase and storage with a realistic borrowing or hire option. Check availability, condition, deposits, responsibilities and return arrangements before relying on shared access. Choose ownership where it serves your needs, but make the comparison before buying equipment for a single short task.

## AU-0605 · Unbundle the advertised package

List every included item and assign value only to the parts you would otherwise buy. Compare the useful subset with buying separately, including delivery and any compatibility requirements. If the bundle pushes you into a larger ongoing commitment or extra storage, count that cost too rather than treating unwanted extras as savings.

## AU-0606 · Read the exact refurbished condition

Ask what 'refurbished', 'renewed' or 'as new' means for the specific item, including cosmetic condition, battery or component checks, accessories and seller support. Obtain the description in writing and compare the total with a new or ordinary second-hand alternative. Do not assume different sellers use the same grading system or perform the same work.

## AU-0607 · Compare repair with replacement using a baseline

Get a diagnosis and written repair estimate, then compare it with a suitable replacement rather than the most expensive upgrade. Include remaining usefulness, downtime, installation and any guarantee attached to the repair, marking uncertainty honestly. Check relevant consumer rights before paying for either option when the fault may still be the seller's responsibility.

## AU-0609 · Read the final checkout total

Before paying, review quantity, size, model, currency, delivery, optional add-ons and recurring commitments on the final screen. Remove anything you did not intend to choose and save the order confirmation. If the total is unexpectedly different, pause and resolve the difference rather than assuming a later email will automatically correct it.

## AU-0610 · Check returns for a fit-dependent purchase

Before buying something you may need to try, read the seller's change-of-mind policy, condition requirements, return location, postage and timing. Keep this separate from rights relating to a faulty or misdescribed product, and ask for unclear details in writing. Include the possible cost and effort of a return when comparing sellers.

## AU-0611 · Let urgency pass before spending beyond plan

If a countdown, sales pitch or limited offer pushes you towards an unplanned purchase, pause and compare the item with your original need and budget. Decide what price and features would justify buying before re-entering the sales conversation. You can choose to buy, but the reason should survive without the pressure of the moment.

## AU-0612 · Identify who will stand behind the sale

Before a substantial order, establish the actual seller, contact route, returns address and the terms that apply, particularly on a marketplace. Verify details independently where something seems inconsistent and keep the seller information with the receipt. A familiar platform name does not by itself answer who will handle a problem with your particular purchase.
