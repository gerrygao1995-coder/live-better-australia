## AU-0199 · Agree on household money before sharing bills

Discuss income, debts, bills and caring commitments before moving in together. Agree how shared expenses will be split, whose names are on contracts, and how the arrangement changes when circumstances change. Write a simple budget and review it together regularly, keeping the arrangement freely agreed and workable.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Free; an hour initially and a short regular review. |
| Potential value | Reduces avoidable bill surprises and unclear expectations. |
| Applies to | Partners forming or reviewing a shared household; use specialist support if discussion is unsafe. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |

## AU-0200 · Get facts about de facto property rights

Get advice before a shared purchase or separation: two years of cohabitation is not a universal property-rights switch. Federal de facto claims have multiple gateways and other requirements; WA has a separate framework. Under the federal framework, financial-order applications ordinarily need to be made within two years of breakdown, with permission required later.

## AU-0201 · Plan parental pay before the arrival date

Check eligibility and claiming rules before planning leave around government payments. For births or adoptions from July 2026, eligible families can receive up to 130 Parental Leave Pay days, subject to work, income, residence and caring rules. Sharing and reserved days apply; separately confirm employer pay and the right to time off.

## AU-0202 · Compare childcare by the gap you will pay

Check CCS eligibility and ask providers for estimates using your attendance and subsidy. Since January 2026, the 3 Day Guarantee offers eligible families at least three days of subsidised care weekly, with a gap fee still payable. It does not guarantee a place; compare sessions, extras, travel and the current subsidy calculation.

## AU-0203 · Use family dispute resolution when appropriate

Ask an accredited family dispute resolution service about parenting arrangements and disclose safety concerns early. Attempting dispute resolution is generally required before seeking parenting orders, with exemptions, and practitioners assess suitability. Understand the difference between a parenting plan and enforceable orders before recording agreement; get advice about existing orders.

## AU-0204 · Make a safety plan with specialist support

Contact specialist support safely if a relationship involves threats, coercion or violence; call 000 for immediate danger. 1800RESPECT offers support on 1800 737 732, text 0458 737 732 and online. Work through trusted contacts, somewhere safe, children, pets and essential items with a support worker, keeping the plan private.

## AU-0205 · Make caring work visible

List the recurring tasks that keep the household running, including appointments, school messages, shopping and planning. Assign responsibility for noticing and completing each task, then review whether the split still works when schedules change. The aim is a practical agreement both people can sustain, with room to renegotiate rather than score every small favour.

## AU-0206 · Agree how to make a large joint purchase

Set a spending level above which both people want a discussion, and agree which costs count as shared. Before a large purchase, compare needs, ownership, borrowing and what happens if circumstances change. Leave time for each person to consider the proposal freely, and get independent advice for a major legal commitment.

## AU-0207 · Write a childcare backup plan

Identify who can safely collect or care for a child when a parent is delayed or the usual service is unavailable. Confirm willingness, contact details and the service's authorisation process, then keep the plan where caregivers can find it. ## AU-0208 · Keep essential child information accessible

Maintain a concise record of emergency contacts, allergies, relevant care instructions and authorised collection arrangements. Share it only with people who need it and keep the current version clear, especially when multiple carers are involved. Ask the child's clinician or service about instructions you do not understand rather than improvising medical advice.

## AU-0209 · Price a return to work as a household decision

Compare expected take-home income with childcare, travel, work clothing and the timetable you will need. Also discuss career continuity, wellbeing and each person's preferences instead of reducing the decision to one week's bill. Use confirmed employer and service information and revisit the plan after a trial period.

## AU-0210 · Plan children's activities around a full term

Before signing up, check fees, equipment, transport, supervision and the time the activity takes across a term. Let the child try an introductory option if available and agree a manageable number of commitments. ## AU-0211 · Set gift expectations before the occasion

Discuss a comfortable budget, whether second-hand or experience gifts are welcome and whether adults need gifts at all. Explain the arrangement early to relatives so nobody relies on an expensive assumption, and offer a simple alternative. ## AU-0212 · Talk about lending money within the family

Before lending, consider whether you could manage if repayment is late or never occurs and whether saying no would be safer. Agree the amount, purpose, repayment expectations and what happens if circumstances change in writing. For substantial sums, get independent legal advice rather than relying on a label such as 'gift for now'.

## AU-0213 · Keep a regular conversation outside logistics

Set aside time to talk about how each person is managing, separate from bills and transport arrangements. Ask what would make the coming week easier and agree one specific change you can try. ## AU-0214 · Plan pet responsibilities before adopting

Make a realistic budget and check the animal's needs with a suitable professional or reputable shelter. ## AU-0215 · Give teenagers a practical money rehearsal

Let a teenager plan a modest, agreed expense such as a day out or part of a weekly grocery shop. Discuss trade-offs and the result without requiring perfect choices, and keep essential needs outside the experiment. Gradually add responsibility while remaining available to explain bills, contracts and misleading sales claims.

## AU-0216 · Check agreement before sharing family stories

Before posting photographs, personal milestones or another person's difficulties, ask whether they are comfortable with that audience. Respect a refusal and revisit old posts when circumstances change, particularly for children who are becoming old enough to express a preference. Use direct sharing where it meets the need with less exposure.
