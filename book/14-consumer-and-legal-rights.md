## AU-0235 · Check consumer guarantees before paying again

Keep purchase evidence and ask the seller for the remedy matching the fault. Major product problems generally allow a refund-or-replacement choice; minor problems usually allow the business to fix them. Warranty expiry does not automatically end consumer guarantees; change-of-mind returns and many private sales have different rules.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Free to raise a complaint; keep reasonable expenses documented. |
| Potential value | May avoid paying to replace a product the seller must remedy. |
| Applies to | Covered consumer purchases; product, seller and type of failure affect rights. |
| Basis | Official rule |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [ACCC: repair, replace, refund, cancel](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/problem-with-a-product-or-service-you-bought/repair-replace-refund-cancel)

## AU-0236 · Spend two dollars before buying a used car

Run your own official A$2 PPSR VIN search before paying for a privately sold used car and save the certificate. Investigate registered security interests and available stolen or written-off records. Separately arrange a mechanical inspection and check registration requirements; a register result is not a roadworthiness guarantee.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | An official online PPSR car search is A$2 at the check date; inspection costs extra. |
| Potential value | A small check can uncover a costly legal or vehicle-history problem. |
| Applies to | Used-car buyers, especially private purchasers; written-off registration rules vary by state. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [PPSR: check before buying a second-hand car](https://www.ppsr.gov.au/newsroom/parents)

## AU-0237 · Escalate a complaint to someone who can resolve it

Complain to the business with evidence and the outcome sought, then use the ACCC directory for the appropriate agency or dispute scheme. TIO handles relevant telecommunications disputes, AFCA financial disputes and jurisdictional schemes energy issues. The ACCC collects enforcement information but does not resolve individual complaints; check each scheme's deadlines.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Many complaint pathways are free; court or tribunal costs differ. |
| Potential value | Avoids repeatedly sending evidence to the wrong organisation. |
| Applies to | Consumers with unresolved disputes; the product, provider and location determine the pathway. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [ACCC: where to go for consumer help](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/problem-with-a-product-or-service-you-bought/where-to-go-for-consumer-help)

## AU-0238 · Move quickly after a suspected scam

Stop scam contact and immediately call your bank or payment provider through a verified channel if money or account access is at risk. Keep evidence, follow Scamwatch's recovery steps and contact IDCARE if identity information is exposed. Beware upfront-fee recovery offers; recovery is not guaranteed.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Reporting and IDCARE support are free; act promptly. |
| Potential value | Can limit further loss and organise the next steps. |
| Applies to | Anyone who suspects a scam; response depends on payment method and information exposed. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Scamwatch: what to do if you have been scammed](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/stop-check-protect/what-to-do-if-youve-been-scammed)

## AU-0239 · Ask about legal help before the deadline

Note every deadline on a dispute or court document and contact legal assistance promptly. Use the Attorney-General's directory to find legal aid, community legal centres or specialist help. Bring a short chronology and key documents, then confirm eligibility, cost and whether advice, document help or representation is available.

| Applies to | People with legal problems; service remit, location and eligibility govern assistance. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |

## AU-0240 · Make a privacy complaint with a clear record

Complain to the organisation first, keeping dates, correspondence and the outcome sought. If there is no response within 30 days or it is unsatisfactory, check OAIC or an approved dispute scheme. Privacy Act coverage has exceptions, and complaints generally should concern matters less than twelve months old.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Making the initial complaint costs only your time; ask the relevant scheme about its process. |
| Potential value | Creates a usable evidence trail and directs the issue to the right body. |
| Applies to | People whose information was handled by a covered agency or organisation; jurisdiction and time limits matter. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [OAIC: before lodging a privacy complaint](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-complaints/lodge-a-privacy-complaint-with-us) · [OAIC: what you can complain about](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-complaints/what-you-can-complain-about)

## AU-0241 · Save the promise that persuaded you to buy

Keep the advertisement, product description, model number and important written answers alongside the receipt. Capture the version you relied on, especially a promised feature or delivery date, before a listing changes. A tidy record helps explain a later discrepancy without relying on memory or repeatedly searching the seller's website.

## AU-0242 · Ask what the extended warranty adds

Before paying extra, compare the warranty document with your existing consumer-guarantee rights and manufacturer cover. Check duration, exclusions, excesses, claim process and who provides the cover; an extended warranty is an additional product, not the source of all your rights. Decide whether its specific extra benefits are worth the price for you.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Reading is free; warranty prices vary. |
| Potential value | Avoids buying cover without understanding its value. |
| Applies to | Consumers offered an extended warranty. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

<a id="au-0243"></a>

## AU-0243 · Check whether a cooling-off right actually applies

Do not assume an ordinary store or online purchase has a statutory change-of-mind period. ACCC guidance describes a ten-business-day cancellation right for qualifying unsolicited consumer agreements, such as certain door-to-door or telemarketing sales. Check the agreement type and start date promptly, then keep proof if you exercise a cancellation right.

<a id="au-0244"></a>

## AU-0244 · Inspect the seller as well as the second-hand item

Establish whether the seller is a business or a private person, inspect the condition and check the model's safety information. ACCC guidance says most consumer guarantees do not apply to private person-to-person sales, while business sales have consumer protections. Be especially careful with safety-critical items or products missing parts, labels or instructions.

## AU-0245 · Check the recall before using a suspect product

If a product appears unsafe or a recall notice names it, confirm the exact brand, model and batch through Product Safety Australia. Follow the recall's instructions for stopping use and obtaining the specified remedy, and keep evidence of the item. Report an unsafe consumer product through the official pathway if it is not already listed.

| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Official recall information is free. |
| Potential value | Reduces exposure to an identified product hazard. |
| Applies to | Owners of consumer products; follow the specific recall notice. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0246 · Request a quote with clear boundaries

Before accepting a repair or service, ask what is included, what is excluded and whether extra work needs your approval. Specify the problem you want addressed and ask how diagnostic fees interact with the final bill. Keep a copy of the quote and approval so an unexpected charge can be discussed against the same document.

## AU-0247 · Choose the remedy you can explain

When complaining, describe the fault, dates, impact and outcome you seek in a few factual paragraphs. Attach the relevant evidence and ask for a clear response rather than starting with insults or a long unrelated history. Keep the remedy realistic and use the appropriate consumer guidance before asserting a particular legal entitlement.

## AU-0248 · Record returns and handovers

Before returning an item, record its condition, accessories and identifying details, then follow the seller's documented return process. Use a method that gives appropriate proof of handover or delivery and retain the reference number. Ask when the item will be assessed and how the outcome will be communicated, particularly for expensive goods.

## AU-0249 · Keep a payment dispute separate from a refund promise

If a business promises a refund, note the amount, method and expected date, then check that it actually arrives. If it does not, contact the business and ask your payment provider about any available dispute process and its deadline. Do not assume an open seller conversation preserves a separate payment-provider deadline.

## AU-0250 · Treat a settlement document as a new decision

Before accepting a settlement, read what it resolves, when payment is due and which claims or disclosures the wording may affect. Ask for clarification and get independent advice if the release is broad or the stakes are high. Keep a signed copy and calendar the promised payment instead of relying on a verbal summary.

## AU-0251 · Keep deadlines in one complaint log

For an unresolved matter, record each contact, promised response date and any external deadline in a single timeline. Put the next action in your calendar and retain the documents supporting it, without sending the whole archive every time. Update the log after a phone call while the details are still fresh.

## AU-0252 · Ask for accessibility in the complaints process

Tell the business or service what would help you participate, such as written communication, an interpreter, a support person or more accessible documents. Explain the practical need and ask how the arrangement will be handled rather than struggling through a format that does not work. Keep a record and seek an appropriate advocate if necessary.
