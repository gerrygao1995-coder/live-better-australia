Getting around well means matching transport to your location, budget and abilities. Compare real journeys and full ownership costs, while keeping maintenance and road safety central.

## AU-0433 · Give a car an annual ownership budget

Add insurance, rego, finance, servicing, tyres, fuel, parking and likely repairs to the purchase price. Compare that total with a realistic mix of public transport, occasional hire and other options before deciding how much vehicle your household can afford.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | A written estimate using local quotes and your travel pattern. |
| Potential value | Shows costs that continue long after the purchase is complete. |
| Applies to | Households buying a first, replacement or additional car. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Moneysmart: Buying and running a car](https://moneysmart.gov.au/student-life-and-money/buying-and-running-a-car)

## AU-0434 · Commission an independent used-car inspection

Arrange an inspection by an appropriate independent mechanic before committing, and ask what the inspection excludes. Read the service history and follow up major findings; a tidy interior, roadworthy certificate or short test drive does not answer every mechanical question.

| Potential value | Adds information about condition and likely repairs to the price comparison. |
| Applies to | Used-car buyers, especially where mechanical knowledge is limited. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Moneysmart: Buying and running a car](https://moneysmart.gov.au/student-life-and-money/buying-and-running-a-car)

## AU-0435 · Run your own official PPSR search

Check the vehicle identification number against the actual vehicle and use the official PPSR search before a used-car purchase. Read the result and retain the certificate; a search can reveal registered security interests and available vehicle-history information but does not establish mechanical condition.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | A small official search fee; avoid unnecessary reseller charges. |
| Potential value | Can identify financial or recorded-history issues before money changes hands. |
| Applies to | Used-car buyers; inspect the certificate and its limitations. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Australian Government: Personal Property Securities Register](https://www.ppsr.gov.au/)

## AU-0436 · Look up outstanding vehicle recalls

Search the official Vehicle Recalls database using accurate make, model and vehicle details. Contact the manufacturer or authorised dealer about any relevant notice and keep the completion record; a previous owner's assurance is less useful than confirmation that the remedy was carried out.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Checking is free; remedy arrangements depend on the recall. |
| Potential value | Helps identify an unresolved safety issue in a vehicle you use. |
| Applies to | Current owners and prospective used-vehicle buyers. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Australian Government: Vehicle recalls](https://www.vehiclerecalls.gov.au/recalls)

## AU-0437 · Separate CTP from property-damage cover

Check exactly what compulsory third-party insurance and your other motor policy cover. Compare third-party property and comprehensive options with excesses and exclusions; compulsory cover should not be assumed to pay for damage you cause to someone else's car or your own.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | A policy review; premiums vary with cover and circumstances. |
| Potential value | Makes a potentially large insurance gap visible before driving. |
| Applies to | Drivers; registration and CTP arrangements vary by jurisdiction. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Moneysmart: Choosing car insurance](https://moneysmart.gov.au/car-insurance/choosing-car-insurance)

## AU-0438 · Compare a car loan with the final payment included

Ask for the total amount repayable, fees, term and any balloon payment in writing. Work out how a final lump sum would be funded and compare finance independently of the dealership's vehicle-price discussion, particularly when a small instalment dominates the sales pitch.

| Cost / effort | Time to compare loan documents and a calculator. |
| Potential value | Shows the full borrowing commitment rather than only the regular payment. |
| Applies to | People financing a car through a lender or dealership. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Moneysmart: Car loans](https://moneysmart.gov.au/loans/car-loans)

## AU-0439 · Use fuel comparisons without an expensive detour

## AU-0440 · Put servicing into the calendar

Follow the manufacturer's service schedule and note the next date or distance requirement. Ask for written approval before additional work, keep invoices and tell the mechanic about emerging symptoms; routine checks are easier to plan than a breakdown on an essential trip.

| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0441 · Check tyres before a long drive

Inspect tyres for visible damage and ask a professional about wear you cannot assess. Check pressures against the manufacturer's guidance, including the spare if carried, and make sure you know what equipment your vehicle has for a puncture.

| Applies to | Drivers preparing for longer or less familiar journeys. |

## AU-0442 · Plan travel around being rested

Build rest and flexible arrival time into a long trip, and avoid starting after a day that has already left you tired. If fatigue develops, stop safely and rest; a cheaper overnight plan is not worth relying on tricks to stay awake.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Time for breaks and possibly accommodation. |
| Potential value | Reduces pressure to keep driving when you are no longer alert. |
| Applies to | All drivers, especially after shift work or disrupted sleep. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Queensland Government: Driving tired](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/safety/road-safety/driving-safely/driving-tired)

## AU-0443 · Trial public transport on an ordinary day

Use the official local trip planner for the time you actually travel, then test the journey if practical. Check accessible stops, transfers, fare rules and the return service, including evening or weekend differences; NSW's planner is one example, not a national timetable.

| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | A trial fare and time to check the route. |
| Potential value | Reveals whether an alternative journey works in daily life. |
| Applies to | People considering a change from driving or moving house. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Transport for NSW: Trip planner](https://transportnsw.info/trip)

## AU-0444 · Check the last service before staying late

Save the final practical train, bus, ferry or coach connection and a backup option before heading out. Include the walk or accessible transfer at both ends, and check service updates rather than assuming yesterday's timetable applies on a weekend or public holiday.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | A brief timetable check before departure. |
| Potential value | Avoids an unexpectedly costly or difficult trip home. |
| Applies to | People relying on scheduled transport, particularly at night. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0449 · Recheck cover when the car's use changes

Tell the insurer about material changes it asks you to disclose, such as drivers, address or use, and request written confirmation. Read conditions before using a personal vehicle for delivery or other paid work; an ordinary policy may have relevant exclusions.

| Potential value | Aligns the policy record with how the vehicle is actually used. |
| Applies to | Drivers changing household arrangements or vehicle use. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0450 · Keep sale and transfer records together

When selling a vehicle, use your jurisdiction's official transfer process and retain dated sale, payment and handover records. Remove personal information from paired devices and navigation systems before handover, and confirm the separate insurance and toll-account changes you need to make.

| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | A short checklist and any official transfer requirements. |
| Potential value | Reduces administrative loose ends after the vehicle leaves your possession. |
| Applies to | Private sellers; transfer duties and deadlines vary locally. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Australian Government: Personal Property Securities Register](https://www.ppsr.gov.au/)
