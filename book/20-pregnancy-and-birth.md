# 20. Pregnancy and birth

Seek early, individual maternity advice and compare local care options. Decisions, medical needs and available services differ; support should respect the pregnant person's preferences.

## AU-0343 · Book a preconception health conversation

If planning pregnancy, ask your GP to review existing conditions, medicines, vaccinations and relevant personal or family history. Bring your questions and discuss what should happen before trying to conceive; planning ahead can create time to adjust care safely.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Consultation and tests may cost A$. |
| Potential value | Identifies decisions best addressed before pregnancy. |
| Applies to | People planning pregnancy; unexpected pregnancy still warrants timely care. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Healthdirect: Planning for your pregnancy](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/planning-for-your-pregnancy)

## AU-0344 · Contact maternity care early

Once you know you are pregnant, contact your GP or local maternity service to understand booking and care options. Ask which appointments and referrals are needed and who will coordinate care; availability, clinical needs and location can limit the options.

## AU-0345 · Compare the full maternity-care cost

Ask for an estimate covering antenatal visits, tests, birth, anaesthesia and follow-up rather than only a provider's headline fee. Check Medicare eligibility and any private insurance waiting periods, exclusions and newborn arrangements before committing to a private pathway.

## AU-0346 · Use non-judgemental pregnancy-options support

If a pregnancy is unplanned or you are uncertain, seek qualified, confidential advice promptly about your available options and timing. In NSW, the Pregnancy Choices Helpline is 1800 008 463 on weekdays; elsewhere ask your GP or state health service for local support.

## AU-0347 · Adapt food choices for pregnancy

Ask your maternity team about a varied diet and foods that need extra food-safety care during pregnancy. Choose affordable suitable foods and discuss persistent nausea, restricted diets or difficulty eating rather than trying to meet needs through a large collection of products.

## AU-0348 · Check supplements before buying a bundle

Ask your GP or midwife which pregnancy supplements are appropriate, including advice about folate and iodine for your circumstances. Show them every product you take so ingredients are not duplicated; high doses and specialist needs require individual clinical advice.

## AU-0349 · Review medicines without stopping them abruptly

Give your maternity clinician a complete list of prescription medicines, non-prescription products and supplements. Ask what should continue or change and when; stopping necessary treatment can also cause harm, so avoid making changes solely because a package mentions pregnancy.

## AU-0350 · Ask for help with alcohol, nicotine or other drug use

Tell your maternity clinician about alcohol, smoking, vaping or other substance use so they can offer appropriate support. Avoid alcohol during pregnancy and discuss safe treatment for dependence; honest disclosure is more useful than trying to manage withdrawal or a quit attempt alone.

## AU-0351 · Act promptly on changed baby movements

Learn your baby's usual movement pattern with guidance from your maternity team. If movements reduce or change, contact your midwife, doctor or maternity service immediately; do not wait until the next appointment or rely on a home device for reassurance.

| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0352 · Know pregnancy warning signs and the contact route

Ask your maternity team which symptoms require urgent review and save the after-hours number. Severe headache, visual changes, upper abdominal pain or feeling suddenly unwell can need urgent assessment; call 000 for a life-threatening emergency rather than waiting for a callback.

| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Healthdirect: Pre-eclampsia](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/pre-eclampsia)

## AU-0356 · Get a clear physical recovery plan

Before leaving maternity care, ask about expected bleeding, wound or perineal care, pain, bladder and bowel concerns, and signs requiring urgent help. Confirm the follow-up appointment and contact number so you know whom to call when recovery differs from the plan.

## AU-0357 · Allocate practical help after birth

Before the baby arrives, agree who can manage meals, shopping, household work and other children's needs. Keep the plan flexible and ask visitors for a useful task; support should create room for recovery rather than another schedule to host.

## AU-0359 · Treat persistent postnatal distress as a care need

Tell your GP, midwife or child-health nurse about anxiety, low mood or difficulty coping after birth, even if things seem fine to other people. Ask for treatment and practical support; immediate danger to yourself or the baby requires 000.

| Applies to | New parents; a professional can assess the pattern of symptoms. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0360 · Seek care and support after pregnancy loss

Get medical advice about pain or bleeding in pregnancy, and follow the review plan after a diagnosed miscarriage. Ask about physical recovery, emotional support and when to seek urgent help; there is no requirement to cope privately or decide future plans immediately.
