# 13. Ageing and bereavement

Small preparations can spare a family difficult decisions during illness or grief. Start with assessed needs, documented wishes and clear responsibilities rather than the first product or service offered.

## AU-0217 · Ask for aged-care support before a crisis

Contact My Aged Care when daily activities become difficult and explain needs and urgency. Support at Home replaced Home Care Packages in November 2025; assessment determines eligibility. Approved personal-care services have no out-of-pocket charge from October 2026, but other contributions vary, so confirm funding, waiting arrangements and your support plan.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Assessment enquiries are free; service contributions vary. |
| Potential value | Earlier access to information and support for living independently. |
| Applies to | People meeting aged-care eligibility; assessment, approved services and funding determine access. |
| Basis | Official rule |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [My Aged Care: Support at Home](https://www.myagedcare.gov.au/support-at-home) · [My Aged Care: apply for an assessment](https://www.myagedcare.gov.au/how-apply-assessment)

## AU-0218 · Review your will and super together

Review your will after major life changes and have any DIY document checked. Separately ask your super fund about eligible beneficiaries, binding nominations and expiry: a will does not automatically control super. Tell a trusted person where documents are stored without publishing account details.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Professional fees vary; fund information is usually available without charge. |
| Potential value | Reduces missing documents and conflicts between wishes and formal arrangements. |
| Applies to | Adults planning an estate; will formalities are state-based and super has separate rules. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Moneysmart: wills and powers of attorney](https://moneysmart.gov.au/plan-for-your-retirement/wills-and-powers-of-attorney)

## AU-0219 · Document who can decide if you cannot

Choose trusted people for financial and healthcare decisions while you can make decisions yourself. The roles, forms, witnessing and authority differ by jurisdiction; use local guidance rather than assuming a partner has every power. Discuss an advance care directive with your doctor and share copies with appropriate people.

## AU-0220 · Get support for the person doing the caring

Contact Carer Gateway on 1800 422 737 if you provide unpaid care for someone with disability, illness or age-related needs. Ask about counselling, peer support, coaching, tailored assistance and respite. Describe the practical support you need and make a plan for times you cannot provide care.

## AU-0221 · Compare the whole aged-care fee arrangement

Request written residential aged-care charges covering care, accommodation and optional extras. Ask which fee arrangements and transitional protections apply, given the November 2025 changes. Use the official estimator and means-assessment process, and seek independent advice before major accommodation or asset decisions; compare care needs and preferences alongside price.

<a id="au-0222"></a>

## AU-0222 · Use a checklist when someone dies

Use the Services Australia checklist and local death-registration process, identify the executor and locate the will. Request a written funeral quote and track notifications; Services Australia asks to be told within 28 days. Ask about surviving-household support and legal help with the estate, sharing tasks with trusted people.

## AU-0223 · Choose help that supports your decisions

Ask My Aged Care about registering a trusted supporter if you want help understanding information and communicating preferences. A supporter can assist with the process; clarify the role rather than assuming it transfers every decision-making power. Choose someone who listens to your wishes and ask how to change the arrangement if needed.

## AU-0224 · Ask for an aged-care decision review promptly

Read the Notice of Decision and ask about review options if the assessment outcome does not reflect your needs. My Aged Care describes a free internal review and later external-review options; check the applicable deadline and evidence requirements immediately. Keep your request, reference number and any new information showing why the decision should change.

<a id="au-0225"></a>

## AU-0225 · Raise a care complaint with an advocate

Write down what happened, its effect and the outcome wanted, then ask for help if you are uncomfortable raising it alone. Government-subsidised aged-care complaints can go to the Complaints Commissioner, and OPAN can support you; you do not have to approach the provider first. Distinguish a service complaint from an assessment review.

## AU-0226 · Pause when someone pressures you about assets

If someone pressures you to change a will, transfer property, lend money or sign authority over your affairs, seek independent advice privately before acting. Moneysmart identifies these as possible signs of elder financial abuse. Speak to a trusted professional or appropriate support service and make a safety plan if raising the issue could put you at risk.

## AU-0227 · Describe care needs with ordinary examples

Before an assessment or review, note what happens on a typical day and on a difficult day: preparing food, showering, transport, medication routines and social contact. Record what help is currently provided and where gaps remain, with the person's consent. Concrete examples make a conversation more useful than a broad statement that everything is 'fine'.

## AU-0228 · Compare home-care providers using the same questions

Ask each provider about availability, worker continuity, service scheduling, cancellation terms, communication and how complaints are handled. Use the same list for every conversation and request the price information in writing. Include what matters to the person receiving care, such as language, cultural preferences or timing, instead of choosing on a single headline rate.

## AU-0229 · Prepare a short hospital-to-home question list

Before discharge, ask the treating team what help is needed, which medicines or instructions changed, who arranges follow-up and who to call if the plan is unclear. Write the answers in plain language and check that the person and carer understand them. Confirm equipment and transport arrangements rather than assuming someone else has organised them.

## AU-0230 · Plan a home-accessibility conversation

Walk through the person's usual routine together and note awkward steps, lighting, storage and tasks that feel unsafe. Ask an appropriate clinician or assessor what changes may help before buying major equipment or commissioning alterations. Include the person's preferences and housing permissions so the proposed changes support how they want to live.

## AU-0231 · Make an emergency caring handover

Prepare a short, current summary of who needs care, essential routines, professional contacts and where authorised information is kept. Agree who could step in if the main carer becomes unavailable and check that they understand the role. Keep medical instructions from the relevant professional, and store the handover securely but accessibly.

## AU-0232 · Record funeral wishes without buying immediately

Write down preferences about ceremony, cultural practices, burial or cremation and who should be consulted. Tell trusted people where to find the note and keep it separate from passwords or sensitive financial records. If considering a prepaid arrangement, compare the contract, inclusions, portability and cancellation terms with independent advice before committing.

## AU-0233 · Give the executor a map of the paperwork

With the chosen executor's agreement, maintain a list of important organisations, advisers, document locations and recurring commitments. Keep the list current after account or household changes without placing passwords in an unsecured document. Ask your legal adviser how the executor should access the necessary records and authority when the time comes.

## AU-0234 · Accept practical help during grief

Choose a few tasks others could handle, such as meals, transport, childcare or organising a contact list, and ask one trusted person to coordinate offers. Keep major non-urgent decisions open until you have the information and support you need. If grief is affecting your ability to manage daily life, contact a health professional or support service.
