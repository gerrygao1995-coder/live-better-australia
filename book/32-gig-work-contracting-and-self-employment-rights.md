## AU-0559 · Check status from the relationship, not the label

Read the contract and describe how the work operates in practice before accepting a claim that you are a contractor. Fair Work explains that different legal tests can apply depending on the business and circumstances; an ABN or invoice alone is not a complete answer. Get advice if the written label and actual arrangement conflict.

## AU-0560 · Question a forced switch to contracting

If asked to leave employment and invoice for the same work, keep the proposal and obtain advice before agreeing. Fair Work prohibits specified sham-contracting conduct, including certain misrepresentations and dismissal threats used to engage someone as a contractor. Whether an arrangement is unlawful depends on the facts; changing paperwork alone does not settle the question.

## AU-0561 · Check contractor super separately

Ask whether the engagement creates a superannuation obligation even if you are otherwise contracting. Government guidance says a hirer may need to pay super when payment is mainly for your labour and skills and you perform the work personally. Structure and other conditions matter, so use ATO guidance or advice rather than assuming an ABN removes the obligation.

## AU-0562 · Act promptly after platform deactivation

Save the deactivation notice, reasons and relevant platform messages, then check Fair Work's employee-like-worker guidance. Eligible workers can seek an unfair-deactivation remedy through the Fair Work Commission, generally within 21 days; eligibility and the kind of deactivation matter. Do not assume an internal platform appeal extends the external deadline.

## AU-0563 · Ask about a disputed contract term

If a service contract seems one-sided, identify the exact wording, practical effect and change you want before seeking advice. Official guidance describes different review pathways through dispute resolution, the Fair Work Commission or courts, with eligibility requirements. Do not simply ignore a term because it feels unfair; ask which law and remedy actually apply.

## AU-0564 · Price the hours nobody pays directly

Track a representative week including finding work, waiting, travel, administration and actual paid tasks. Subtract your identified work costs before comparing the result with another job, keeping taxes and personal circumstances separate for proper advice. Use your own recorded figures rather than treating another worker's best shift as a typical earning rate.

## AU-0565 · Write the deliverable before quoting

Describe what you will provide, the required inputs, the deadline and how completion will be assessed. Ask the client to confirm assumptions and exclusions before the quote is accepted, especially where 'a few changes' could mean very different amounts of work. ## AU-0566 · Agree how changes will be approved

Set a process for requests outside the agreed scope, including who can approve extra cost and any effect on timing. When a change arrives, describe it and obtain confirmation before performing substantial additional work. Keep the relationship courteous while making it easy for both sides to see what was included and what changed.

## AU-0567 · Use milestones that can be checked

For a larger job, propose stages with clear outputs, review points and payment dates rather than one vague final deadline. Confirm what the client must supply and what happens if feedback is delayed. A staged plan does not guarantee payment, but it makes progress and emerging problems easier to discuss before too much work accumulates.

## AU-0568 · Confirm the person authorised to approve work

Ask who may approve the quote, variations and final acceptance, and obtain the correct legal and billing details for the client. Keep instructions from that person or the agreed channel rather than relying on a casual request from someone nearby. Clarify any change of contact before continuing a material new task.

## AU-0569 · Read cancellation and termination terms early

Before accepting work, check how either party can end the arrangement, what notice is required and what happens to completed work, deposits and expenses. Ask for unclear terms to be explained and obtain advice on disproportionate or high-stakes obligations. Plan for an interruption rather than treating the expected project duration as guaranteed income.

## AU-0570 · Check insurance for the work you actually do

Describe the actual tasks, vehicle use, locations, clients and subcontracting arrangements to an insurer or adviser, then ask for confirmation of relevant cover and exclusions. Do not assume a personal policy or a platform's summary covers every incident. Keep the current policy and renewal date with your work records.

## AU-0571 · Separate client expenses from your fee

Agree which travel, materials, bookings or other expenses the client will reimburse and what approval or receipts are needed. Confirm any limit before spending and show reimbursable items clearly on the invoice. If the client changes the task, revisit the expense agreement rather than assuming the original permission covers every new cost.

Agree your next follow-up date and obtain advice before threats, debt collection or legal action. ## AU-0574 · Make safe-work expectations part of the booking

Before attending a site or delivery, ask about access, hazards, equipment and who to contact if conditions differ from the booking. ## AU-0575 · Check whether subcontracting is permitted

Before asking someone else to perform part of the work, review the client contract, confidentiality obligations, required qualifications and insurance arrangements. Agree responsibilities and payment in writing and get advice on any employment or other obligations you may create. A helpful informal handover can still leave important responsibilities unresolved if nobody checks them.

## AU-0576 · Review whether the arrangement still works

At an agreed interval, compare actual earnings, workload, client concentration, late payments and wellbeing with the assumptions behind your quote. Decide whether to change pricing, scope, availability or the kinds of work you accept, using your own records. Discuss contractual notice requirements before ending an engagement and keep a practical transition plan.
