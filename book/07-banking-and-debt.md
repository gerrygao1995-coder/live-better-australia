Aim for a system you can understand and maintain. Start with cash needed for essentials, understand the commitments already in place, and ask for help early when repayments no longer fit.

## AU-0109 · Read the conditions behind the savings rate

Compare the savings rate you can actually receive after fees and bonus conditions. Check introductory expiry dates, withdrawal rules and linked-account requirements. Keep near-term money accessible, and use APRA's official deposit checker linked by Moneysmart when checking protection; bank brands and institutions are not always equivalent.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | A short review; avoid opening accounts with conditions you cannot reasonably meet. |
| Potential value | Can improve interest received and reduce avoidable account costs. |
| Applies to | People with savings or a cash buffer; no particular bank is recommended. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Moneysmart: Savings accounts](https://moneysmart.gov.au/banking/savings-accounts)

## AU-0110 · Build a buffer at a pace you can afford

Choose an achievable first buffer target and transfer only what remains affordable after essentials and required repayments. Keep it separate from daily spending and adjust for variable income. When an emergency uses the money, rebuild when possible; using the buffer for its purpose is part of the plan.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Whatever amount is affordable after essentials and required repayments. |
| Potential value | May reduce the need to borrow when an urgent expense arrives. |
| Applies to | People with room to save; seek support first if basics are already unaffordable. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Moneysmart: Save for an emergency fund](https://moneysmart.gov.au/saving/save-for-an-emergency-fund)

## AU-0111 · Give credit card repayments a clear order

List balances, interest rates, minimum payments and due dates. Cover required payments and direct affordable extra repayments towards one chosen card, using a calculator to compare approaches. If minimum payments are already unaffordable, seek hardship assistance instead of adding another loan or forcing an unrealistic repayment schedule.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | A short inventory and regular repayments within your budget. |
| Potential value | Turns a vague debt problem into visible balances and next steps. |
| Applies to | Credit card borrowers; urgent housing or essential bills may require different priorities. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Moneysmart: Pay off your credit card](https://moneysmart.gov.au/credit-cards/pay-off-your-credit-card)

<a id="au-0112"></a>

## AU-0112 · Ask for hardship assistance early

Contact your lender's hardship team early and explain your circumstances and affordable repayments. The lender must consider the request, but the assistance depends on your situation. Ask about interest, loan-term and future-payment effects, retain the written outcome and seek free financial counselling if needed.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | No fee to ask; interest and repayment consequences depend on the arrangement. |
| Potential value | Earlier contact can preserve more options and reduce uncertainty. |
| Applies to | People struggling with credit or loan repayments. |
| Basis | Official rule |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Moneysmart: Financial hardship](https://moneysmart.gov.au/managing-debt/financial-hardship)

## AU-0113 · Check your credit report without buying a repair package

Request free reports from the recognised reporting bodies linked by Moneysmart and check accounts, applications and personal details. Ask for errors to be corrected and investigate unfamiliar activity promptly. You do not need a paid credit-repair package to request corrections, or a new debt to chase a score.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Free reports are available under the access rules; allow time for checking. |
| Potential value | May uncover errors or fraud before a major application. |
| Applies to | People with a credit history, or anyone concerned about identity misuse. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Moneysmart: Credit scores and credit reports](https://moneysmart.gov.au/managing-debt/credit-scores-and-credit-reports) · [Moneysmart: Credit repair](https://moneysmart.gov.au/managing-debt/credit-repair)

## AU-0114 · Count every buy now pay later instalment

List every remaining instalment across all buy now pay later services against upcoming paydays. Check account, late and processing fees and the account used for deductions. Consider the full purchase price before adding another commitment, and ask the provider for hardship assistance when payments no longer fit.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | A short account review; existing fees depend on the provider and terms. |
| Potential value | Makes overlapping commitments visible before they overwhelm a pay cycle. |
| Applies to | Buy now pay later users and anyone considering an instalment purchase. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Moneysmart: Buy now pay later services](https://moneysmart.gov.au/other-ways-to-borrow/buy-now-pay-later-services)

## AU-0115 · Choose an everyday account around your use

Review account-keeping, ATM and other transaction fees against how you actually bank. Compare a suitable low-fee option and check any deposit conditions; a fee waiver tied to regular income may not suit a household with changing pay.

| Potential value | Can reduce charges that repeat without adding useful features. |
| Applies to | People paying fees for routine banking services. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0116 · Move banking with a transition checklist

List salary deposits, benefits, direct debits and recurring card payments before changing accounts. Update each through its official channel and check the next billing cycle before closing the old account, so an otherwise useful switch does not interrupt an essential payment.

## AU-0117 · Keep joint-account access intentional

Agree which shared expenses an account will cover and how much each person contributes. Read who can withdraw money or incur debt, and keep access to information you need; sharing an account should be a considered arrangement, with a plan for changes.

## AU-0118 · Check the total cost before consolidating debt

Compare the total interest, fees and repayment term of a proposed consolidated loan with existing debts. Ask whether previously unsecured debts would become secured against a home or car, and get independent help if the new arrangement puts an essential asset at risk.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | A calculation; independent advice may be useful. |
| Potential value | Avoids mistaking a smaller instalment for a cheaper overall loan. |
| Applies to | People offered debt consolidation or refinancing services. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Moneysmart: Debt consolidation and refinancing](https://moneysmart.gov.au/managing-debt/debt-consolidation-and-refinancing)

## AU-0119 · Treat a guarantee as a possible debt

Before guaranteeing someone else's loan, read the contract and obtain independent advice about the maximum liability and any security. Work out what repaying it would mean for your own housing and essentials; a close relationship does not reduce the contractual risk.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Legal advice may cost; never rush the decision. |
| Potential value | Makes the worst-case obligation concrete before signing. |
| Applies to | People asked to guarantee a family member's or friend's borrowing. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Moneysmart: Going guarantor on a loan](https://moneysmart.gov.au/loans/going-guarantor-on-a-loan)

## AU-0121 · Keep a debt-collection contact log

Record dates, the collector's identity, requests and what was agreed. Save messages and use the official complaint pathway if conduct is unacceptable; if court documents arrive, seek legal advice promptly rather than relying on an informal complaint to pause deadlines.

## AU-0122 · Stress-test repayments against a lean month

Use a low-income month rather than your best month when checking a new borrowing commitment. Include essentials and existing repayments first, then consider whether the proposed payment remains manageable without repeatedly borrowing again or relying on an uncertain bonus.

| Applies to | Casual workers and households with variable or seasonal income. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0123 · Give a lump sum a temporary holding plan

Before spending a refund, back pay or other one-off amount, list urgent bills, debt obligations and near-term needs. Allow time to understand any tax or benefit consequences and make deliberate allocations, rather than treating a temporary balance as new ongoing income.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | A written allocation plan; specialist advice where needed. |
| Potential value | Can make a one-off receipt useful for more than one immediate demand. |
| Applies to | People receiving irregular income or an unexpected payment. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Moneysmart: How to do a budget](https://moneysmart.gov.au/budgeting/how-to-do-a-budget)

## AU-0124 · Check promotional debt expiry dates

For an interest-free or balance-transfer offer, record the fee, expiry date, revert rate and payment conditions. Calculate the repayment needed to clear it within the offer if affordable, and avoid adding purchases whose treatment you have not checked in the terms.

| Potential value | Makes the end of a temporary offer visible well in advance. |
| Applies to | Borrowers using a promotional credit arrangement. |

## AU-0125 · Ask what a payment pause really changes

If a lender offers reduced or deferred payments, request a written explanation of interest, arrears, the remaining term and later repayments. Compare the arrangement with your expected recovery in income, and flag any payment you cannot realistically make before accepting.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | A discussion with the lender's hardship team. |
| Potential value | Helps turn immediate relief into an arrangement you understand. |
| Applies to | People negotiating temporary relief on an existing loan. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0126 · Use a bills account without overdrawing it

If separate accounts help you, assign a bills account and fund it from a realistic schedule. Turn on useful low-balance alerts, check upcoming deductions and leave enough for essentials; automation still needs an occasional review when prices or income change.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Account terms vary; set-up takes a short session. |
| Potential value | Can make committed money easier to distinguish from available spending. |
| Applies to | People who prefer separate accounts for household cash flow. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |
