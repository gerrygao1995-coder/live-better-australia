# 6. Renting and buying a home

Housing choices affect cash flow, time and security. Compare the full cost and keep good records. Rental and property law depends on the state or territory and the type of arrangement.

## AU-0091 · Price the home as a place to live

Compare rent plus transport, utilities, internet, parking and moving costs. Separately record cash needed for the bond and any overlapping homes. Inspect practical features and accessibility, then read the lease and confirm its length suits your plans before committing.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | An inspection and a simple written estimate. |
| Potential value | A clearer view of affordability before a binding commitment. |
| Applies to | Prospective renters; tenancy rules and bond limits vary by jurisdiction. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Moneysmart: Rental bonds and leases](https://moneysmart.gov.au/student-life-and-money/rental-bonds-and-leases)

## AU-0092 · Make a rental evidence folder on day one

Photograph rooms, existing damage and faults before unpacking. Complete the condition report within your jurisdiction's deadline, keeping a copy and proof of return. Store the lease, bond receipt and repair correspondence together, then take another dated set of photos when leaving.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Time at move-in and move-out; a phone camera is usually sufficient. |
| Potential value | Better evidence if there is a dispute about damage or your bond. |
| Applies to | Renters; check the required form and return deadline where you live. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Moneysmart: Rental bonds and leases](https://moneysmart.gov.au/student-life-and-money/rental-bonds-and-leases)

## AU-0093 · Use the tenancy rules for your address

Save the official renting page for your state or territory and use its rules for repairs, notices and disputes. Keep written correspondence and seek local tenancy advice before withholding rent or arranging work yourself. Share houses, boarding arrangements and other housing categories may have different rules.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | A short read now; advice may require an appointment. |
| Potential value | Helps avoid acting on a rule from another state or the wrong housing category. |
| Applies to | All renters; use the jurisdiction and tenancy type that actually apply. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [myGov: Signing a rental lease and state tenancy links](https://my.gov.au/en/services/living-arrangements/finding-renting-and-buying-a-home/renting-a-home/signing-a-rental-lease)

## AU-0094 · Treat a bond claim as a process with deadlines

Check your local bond authority's refund and dispute procedure, read notices promptly and retain evidence. NSW disputes use the prescribed Fair Trading and NCAT process, with short response periods. Follow your own jurisdiction's instructions before agreeing to deductions or assuming a claim will resolve itself.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Time to check notices; tribunal fees or concessions depend on the case. |
| Potential value | Protects your opportunity to challenge an unsupported claim. |
| Applies to | NSW example; other jurisdictions have their own bond authority and procedure. |
| Basis | Official rule |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [NSW Government: Dealing with bond disputes for tenants](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/renting-a-place-to-live/residential-rental-bonds/dealing-bond-disputes-for-tenants)

## AU-0095 · Budget beyond the house deposit

Budget for conveyancing, inspections, applicable transfer duty and ongoing insurance, rates, maintenance and strata costs. Have a solicitor or conveyancer review the contract and check finance and inspection conditions before signing or bidding. Confirm any first-home concession directly; a low deposit does not establish ongoing affordability.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Professional advice and inspections cost money; include them in the buying budget. |
| Potential value | Reduces the chance of an unaffordable or poorly understood purchase. |
| Applies to | Prospective buyers; property rules and concessions vary by state or territory. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Moneysmart: Buying a house](https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans/buying-a-house)

## AU-0096 · Compare mortgages over the same term

Compare rates, fees and features for the same loan amount and term, then test higher-rate repayments. Include switching and possible break costs when refinancing, and ask how offset or redraw access works. Compare total interest: a smaller monthly payment can result from extending the debt.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Time to compare; refinancing and advice may have fees. |
| Potential value | Helps identify the real cost of a loan and the trade-offs of switching. |
| Applies to | Home buyers and borrowers reviewing an existing mortgage. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Moneysmart: Choosing a home loan](https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans/choosing-a-home-loan) · [Moneysmart: Switching home loans](https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-loans/switching-home-loans)

<a id="au-0097"></a>

## AU-0097 · Write a housemate agreement

Agree in writing how rent, bills, cleaning and shared purchases will work, including what happens when someone leaves. Check whose names are on the lease and obtain local advice about responsibility; a private agreement between housemates may not change obligations to the landlord.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | One calm conversation before money becomes disputed. |
| Potential value | Makes expectations visible and provides a record to revisit. |
| Applies to | Share houses; legal responsibilities depend on local tenancy arrangements. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [NSW Fair Trading — sharing a residential rental property (NSW example)](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/rules/sharing-a-residential-rental-property)

## AU-0098 · Test the commute at the relevant time

Before choosing between homes, try or realistically map the trip at your usual starting and finishing times. Include transfers, parking, mobility needs and the last available service, then add those costs to the housing comparison rather than using distance alone.

| Potential value | Helps avoid a cheaper address creating expensive daily travel. |
| Applies to | Prospective renters or buyers with regular travel commitments. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0100 · Ask about an embedded electricity network

Before signing for an apartment or park home, ask who supplies electricity and whether you can choose a retailer. Request written tariffs and fees and include them in your housing budget; ordinary comparison results may not describe the options for that property.

## AU-0102 · Get a contents estimate room by room

List what replacing your possessions would cost after a serious loss, including clothing, furniture and equipment. Check any contents cover against that list, especially item limits and portable valuables; the building owner's insurance should not be assumed to protect your belongings.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | An inventory; insurance premiums depend on cover. |
| Potential value | Helps identify both underinsurance and cover you do not need. |
| Applies to | Renters and owners reviewing household possessions. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Moneysmart: Contents insurance](https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-insurance/contents-insurance)

<a id="au-0104"></a>

## AU-0104 · Price insurance before exchanging contracts

Seek insurance information for the exact property before becoming committed to it. Ask your conveyancer when your responsibility for risk starts and read exclusions and excesses carefully; a property's purchase price does not reveal the cost or availability of suitable insurance.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Quotes are usually free; legal review may cost. |
| Potential value | Avoids discovering a material insurance issue too late. |
| Applies to | Buyers, particularly in areas exposed to flood, bushfire or storms. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0107 · Keep a home maintenance log

Record service dates, warranties and emerging problems for important systems such as hot water, drainage and roofing. Use the manufacturer's instructions and qualified trades for work requiring expertise, and separate urgent safety work from cosmetic improvements when setting the household budget.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | A simple log plus necessary maintenance costs. |
| Potential value | Helps plan expenditure and retain evidence of completed work. |
| Applies to | Home owners; renters should report relevant problems through their process. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Moneysmart: Choosing home insurance](https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-insurance/choosing-home-insurance)

## AU-0108 · Check removalist quotes on the same basis

Give each removalist the same inventory, access conditions and distance, then compare inclusions, insurance arrangements and extra charges. Confirm dates and responsibilities in writing, and keep essential documents and items you need immediately accessible during the move.
