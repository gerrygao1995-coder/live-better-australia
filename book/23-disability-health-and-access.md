# 23. Disability health and access

Health care should work with the person’s communication, access needs and choices. NDIS funding and health-system responsibilities are distinct, and eligibility must be checked for the actual support.

## AU-0397 · Request access adjustments when booking

Tell the health service which practical adjustments you need, such as step-free access, a suitable examination space, a quieter waiting area or a longer appointment. Ask the service to confirm what it can arrange before travelling. Describe your own needs; a diagnosis alone does not tell staff how to make care accessible.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | A$0 to request adjustments; appointment and transport costs vary. |
| Potential value | A better chance of receiving care in a usable setting. |
| Applies to | People with disability or access requirements. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Australian Commission: Intellectual disability and inclusive health care](https://www.safetyandquality.gov.au/clinical-topics/intellectual-disability-and-inclusive-health-care)

## AU-0398 · Tell staff how you communicate best

Explain your preferred communication method and bring any device, communication book or supporter you choose. Ask clinicians to speak directly with you, allow time for answers and provide information in an accessible format. Confirm important instructions in your own way before leaving.

| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0399 · Ask for support to make your own decisions

Request information about options and risks in a form you understand. Involve a trusted supporter if wanted and ask for time to consider choices. Consent arrangements depend on circumstances and local law; disability alone does not determine decision-making capacity.

## AU-0400 · Get new symptoms assessed on their own merits

Explain what is usual for you and what has changed, including pain, function, behaviour or sleep. Ask the clinician to assess the new concern rather than assuming it is explained by an existing disability. A short baseline description can help staff who have not met you before.

| Basis | Official / professional guidance |

## AU-0402 · Check the handover before leaving hospital

Ask for an accessible discharge summary, updated medicine instructions and a clear follow-up plan. Confirm that the next provider and chosen supporters have the information they need and that essential equipment or assistance is arranged. <a id="au-0404"></a>

## AU-0404 · Separate health treatment from NDIS supports

Ask whether a proposed service belongs with the health system or is an eligible disability-related NDIS support. Diagnosis and clinical treatment generally remain health-system responsibilities; an NDIS plan is not general medical insurance. Check the current rules and your plan before assuming a health-related purchase can be reimbursed.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | A$0 for clarification; uncovered treatment may cost money. |
| Potential value | Fewer funding disputes and unexpected bills. |
| Applies to | NDIS participants considering health-related spending. |
| Basis | Official rule |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [NDIS: Disability-related health supports](https://ndis.gov.au/participants/using-your-funding/other-types-support/what-disability-related-health-support)

## AU-0405 · Gather evidence before requesting new supports

Discuss the functional need with your treating professional and ask your NDIS contact which evidence or assessment is required. Keep the request specific about the support, its purpose and how it relates to your disability. Wait for the relevant funding decision before committing to costs you cannot otherwise cover.

| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0406 · Trial equipment against everyday tasks

## AU-0408 · Give occupational therapy a concrete goal

Describe a daily task you want to make easier, such as showering, cooking, getting dressed or managing study. Ask the occupational therapist to assess the task and environment with you, then compare training, equipment and modifications. Agree on how you will judge whether the intervention helps before buying equipment.

| Potential value | Support aimed at a meaningful daily activity. |
| Applies to | People having difficulty with everyday tasks. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0409 · Seek an accessible movement plan

Tell a physiotherapist about your current function, pain, fatigue, access barriers and the activity you want to do. Ask for exercises or movement options you can practise safely in your usual environment. Review the plan when symptoms or needs change instead of following a generic routine indefinitely.

## AU-0410 · Get swallowing difficulties professionally assessed

Tell a GP or speech pathologist about coughing during meals, food sticking or other swallowing concerns. Seek advice before independently changing food textures or thickening drinks, because the right approach depends on assessment. Call 000 for a choking emergency or serious breathing difficulty.

| Applies to | People with swallowing difficulties and their supporters. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Healthdirect: Speech pathology](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/speech-pathology)

Use the Hearing Services Program's eligibility information or ask a participating provider to check. ## AU-0412 · Compare hearing devices by real needs

If eligible for the Hearing Services Program, ask to compare a fully subsidised option with any device that requires a gap payment. Request a written quote including trial, return, warranty and maintenance arrangements. Explain the situations where hearing is difficult so extra features can be judged against a practical need.

## AU-0414 · Raise continence concerns without waiting for a crisis

Tell a GP or continence professional about leakage, urgency or difficulty controlling bladder or bowel function. Describe patterns and the effects on sleep, travel or daily activities. Seek assessment rather than simply increasing product purchases or reducing drinks, and ask about support and product assistance relevant to your circumstances.

| --- | --- |

| Potential value | Potential treatment and more suitable ongoing support. |
| Applies to | People experiencing continence difficulties. |

| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |
