## AU-0577 · Check your electoral enrolment

If eligible, enrol and keep your address current through the Australian Electoral Commission. Enrolment is compulsory for Australian citizens aged eighteen and over who meet the residence criteria; young people can enrol at sixteen or seventeen so they are ready at eighteen. Use the AEC's eligibility and assistance information rather than assuming a parent handles it.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Free through the official AEC service. |
| Potential value | One essential civic record in your control. |
| Applies to | Eligible Australian citizens; the AEC explains exceptions and special circumstances. |
| Basis | Official rule |

## AU-0578 · Ask about your own Medicare card

If you are enrolled in Medicare and at least fifteen, you can request your own card and number. Check Services Australia's online eligibility and alternative process, since the online route has extra conditions. Confirm your contact and bank details through the official service and ask how any existing representative arrangements are handled.

## AU-0579 · Find an existing TFN before applying again

Check whether you already have a tax file number, using ATO online services or the official recovery process if needed. A first application is free, and you only need to apply once; the appropriate identity-check route depends on your circumstances. Keep the number private and provide it only through a legitimate, relevant process.

## AU-0580 · Do not assume eighteen means independent for Youth Allowance

Check the specific student or apprentice payment rules before budgeting on an independent rate. Services Australia generally treats people as permanently independent from twenty-two, with other pathways available in particular circumstances. Turning eighteen or moving out is not by itself a complete eligibility assessment; ask what evidence and income information apply to you.

## AU-0581 · Build a small personal-document folder

Locate your identity documents, education records and essential account references, and find out how to replace anything missing. Keep secure copies where appropriate and record expiry dates without putting sensitive numbers in a public note. Ask a trusted adult to explain unfamiliar documents while keeping track of what belongs to you and where originals are stored.

## AU-0583 · Choose a next-year plan, not a lifetime label

Write two or three realistic options for the next twelve months and compare cost, location, commitments and what you would learn from each. Include a review date and a fallback if the first option does not work. You can make a serious choice without promising that the same occupation or course must suit you forever.

## AU-0584 · Give a gap year a purpose and a budget

Before committing, decide what the year is meant to provide: work experience, saving, caring time, travel or a clearer study choice. Set a spending plan, a few useful milestones and the dates for any future applications or deferred place. Revisit the plan regularly so the year remains intentional rather than drifting past important deadlines.

## AU-0585 · Rehearse the cost of moving out

Build a sample month covering rent, utilities, groceries, transport and irregular purchases, using actual local options where possible. Add the initial costs and a fallback if income changes, then compare staying, sharing or moving. ## AU-0586 · Compare student housing beyond the weekly price

Ask what the quoted amount includes, the contract length, cancellation conditions, transport and practical access to study facilities. Check cooking, laundry, privacy and accessibility needs, and inspect or use a trusted viewing process before committing. A convenient advertised room still needs to suit the way you will live and the period you actually need it.

## AU-0590 · Read anything signed in your name

Before agreeing to a phone plan, shared booking, loan or other contract, identify what you must pay, how long it lasts and how it ends. Ask for unfamiliar wording to be explained and take time to decide, even if a friend says the arrangement is only a favour. Get advice before accepting a significant obligation for someone else.

## AU-0591 · Make a first-pay allocation before spending it

Compare the plan with the actual amount received and revise it after a few pay cycles. Identify people you can approach for different questions, such as a teacher, family member, community worker or experienced colleague. Ask for help with a specific decision and compare advice with official requirements where money or legal obligations are involved. ## AU-0593 · Check age rules for the exact service

Before assuming a birthday changes every entitlement, check the relevant service's age, residence, study and other conditions. Different systems use different thresholds, and a rule for one payment, licence or account does not establish another. Save the official requirement and any appointment or application deadline in your own planning notes.

## AU-0594 · Review the first three months honestly

After starting work, study or a move, compare the reality with your expectations about money, energy, support and interest. Identify one or two adjustments and ask for help early where the plan is not working, while checking any contract or course deadlines. A review is information for the next step, not a verdict on your ability.
