# 28. Retirement transitions

Retirement involves time, housing, relationships and administration as well as money. Use your own needs and the current rules to plan; there is no single balance or retirement age that fits everyone.

## AU-0487 · Sketch the week you want in retirement

Write a possible ordinary week including relationships, interests, care responsibilities, exercise and unstructured time. Compare it with your current routine and try one part before leaving work, so planning covers how you want to spend your time as well as your savings.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | A conversation and a realistic trial of one activity. |
| Potential value | Makes lifestyle priorities concrete enough to budget and plan around. |
| Applies to | People approaching retirement or reducing paid work. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Moneysmart: Make a retirement plan](https://moneysmart.gov.au/plan-for-your-retirement/make-a-retirement-plan)

## AU-0488 · Build a retirement budget from real spending

Start with recent household spending and adjust items likely to change, including commuting, healthcare, travel and housing. Separate essentials from flexible goals and allow for irregular replacements, rather than assuming every expense will fall as soon as employment ends.

## AU-0489 · Distinguish pension age from super access

Check the current rules for accessing your own super and the separate Age Pension requirements. Ask the fund or Services Australia about your circumstances before setting a resignation date; reaching one milestone does not automatically establish eligibility for the other.

## AU-0492 · Discuss retirement timing with your household

Talk through whether partners will stop work together, how shared costs will be paid and what each person wants from the change. Include unpaid caring work and individual spending needs, then record decisions that affect both rather than relying on assumptions.

## AU-0493 · Test a reduced-work arrangement

If it suits your health and circumstances, explore whether fewer hours or a different role is available. Compare the net income, super and lifestyle implications before agreeing, and check any transition-to-retirement product separately rather than treating it as a requirement to work less.

| Potential value | May provide a practical transition instead of one abrupt change. |
| Applies to | Workers considering a gradual retirement; arrangements require agreement. |

## AU-0494 · Compare super withdrawal options before choosing

Ask your fund to explain available lump-sum and income-stream options, fees, access and tax treatment for your circumstances. Consider how each would support spending over time, and seek personal advice for a major choice rather than withdrawing everything merely because it becomes accessible.

| Cost / effort | A fund discussion; tailored advice may have a fee. |
| Potential value | Helps connect withdrawal choices with ongoing income needs. |
| Applies to | People who meet a condition of release for super. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Moneysmart: Getting your super](https://moneysmart.gov.au/how-super-works/getting-your-super)

## AU-0495 · Check housing access beyond the front door

Assess stairs, bathroom access, maintenance and the route to shops, healthcare and people you see regularly. Price practical modifications and support alongside moving costs, so the decision to remain or relocate reflects daily functioning as well as the property's size.

| Potential value | Identifies obstacles that can matter more with changing mobility. |
| Applies to | People planning where to live during retirement. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0496 · Calculate the net proceeds of downsizing

Subtract selling, buying, moving and repair costs from the expected sale proceeds. Ask about pension, tax and super effects before committing, and consider how the new location changes support networks; a lower purchase price does not describe the whole outcome.

| Potential value | Shows how much money and practical benefit a move may actually release. |
| Applies to | Home owners considering a smaller or differently located home. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0501 · Update the will when circumstances change

Review your will after major changes in family, assets or intentions and use appropriate legal help. Confirm the executor is willing to act and knows where the valid document is stored, while checking super nominations separately because they have their own rules.

## AU-0503 · Set boundaries around family financial help

Before gifting, lending or guaranteeing money, check what you can afford and how the arrangement affects your own needs. Get independent advice and document the agreement where appropriate; if you feel pressured, use an elder-abuse or financial-support service before proceeding.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | A deliberate pause and potentially legal or financial advice. |
| Potential value | Protects your choices and resources for future living and care. |
| Applies to | People asked to provide significant financial help to family or others. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0504 · Review the plan after the first year

Compare actual retirement spending and activities with the plan, then adjust what has proved unrealistic. Recheck major assumptions after changes in health, household income or housing, and seek advice before making a large irreversible response to a short-term market movement.

| Cost / effort | A periodic review with current records. |
| Potential value | Keeps a retirement plan connected to life as it actually unfolds. |
| Applies to | Retirees and households supporting an ongoing retirement plan. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |
