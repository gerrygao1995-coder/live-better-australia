## AU-0361 · Set up a simple safe sleep space

Follow Red Nose guidance: place your baby on their back, keep head and face uncovered, and use a safe cot with a firm flat mattress and clear sleep space. Use their own safe sleep space in your room for the recommended early months, and share the guidance with all carers.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | A safe cot and mattress cost A$; extras unnecessary. |
| Potential value | Reduces avoidable hazards during infant sleep. |
| Applies to | Infants; ask for individual advice for medical circumstances. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Red Nose: Six safe sleep recommendations](https://rednose.org.au/safe-sleep-and-safer-pregnancy/pregnancy-to-birth/six-safe-sleep-recommendations/)

## AU-0362 · Keep sleep products separate from safety claims

Before buying a positioner, pillow or other infant sleep accessory, compare it with Red Nose's recommended safe sleep environment. Ask a child-health professional if marketing conflicts with that guidance; do not add products merely because they promise longer sleep.

## AU-0363 · Ask for breastfeeding help early

If breastfeeding is painful, attachment is difficult or you are worried about milk transfer, ask a midwife, child-health nurse or qualified lactation professional to observe a feed. Discuss the baby's growth and your own wellbeing rather than relying only on general online tips.

| Applies to | Breastfeeding parents; complex needs may require specialist care. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Royal Children's Hospital: Breastfeeding](https://www.rch.org.au/kidsinfo/fact_sheets/Breastfeeding/)

## AU-0365 · Introduce solids when developmentally ready

Ask your child-health professional about starting solids around the recommended stage and suitable textures. Offer varied iron-rich foods while continuing appropriate milk feeds, and supervise eating closely; readiness and individual medical needs matter more than comparing your baby with another child.

## AU-0366 · Discuss food-allergy concerns before major exclusions

Discuss suspected food allergy with your child's doctor before removing multiple foods. Ask how to introduce foods safely and maintain nutrition. Call 000 for a severe allergic reaction; do not try the suspected food again at home.

## AU-0367 · Offer toddler meals without constant negotiation

Offer a variety of suitable foods at regular meal and snack times, allowing for changing appetite. Keep the atmosphere calm and discuss persistent feeding difficulty, restricted intake or growth concerns with a health professional rather than escalating pressure at every meal.

## AU-0368 · Take a young baby's fever seriously

Arrange urgent medical assessment for fever in a baby under three months, even if they otherwise seem well. Tell the clinician the baby's age and condition; for breathing difficulty, unresponsiveness or other severe illness, call 000.

## AU-0369 · Watch hydration when a child is unwell

Notice reduced drinking, fewer wet nappies or trips to the toilet, unusual sleepiness and other changes during vomiting, diarrhoea or fever. Ask a clinician about appropriate fluids and seek help promptly if your child cannot drink or seems seriously unwell.

## AU-0370 · Have a safe plan for overwhelming crying

If crying leaves you overwhelmed, put the baby safely in their cot and take a short break while arranging support. Never shake a baby; seek medical advice if crying is unusual or there are illness signs, and ask for help with your own distress.

## AU-0372 · Get help for painful stooling during toilet learning

Discuss painful or infrequent stools and withholding with your child's GP rather than treating accidents as misbehaviour. Make the toilet setup comfortable and follow the agreed treatment plan; persistent constipation may need more than simply adding fibre.

## AU-0376 · Check the floor from a toddler's height

## AU-0378 · Assign one adult to active water supervision

At baths, pools or water play, make it explicit which adult is watching and stay within reach of young children. Put phones aside, empty small water containers after use and restrict unsupervised access; swimming lessons and flotation toys do not replace supervision.

| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | A$0 supervision; barriers and lessons cost extra. |
| Potential value | Reduces everyday drowning hazards. |
| Applies to | All young children, including confident swimmers. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Royal Children's Hospital: Water safety](https://www.rch.org.au/kidsinfo/fact_sheets/Water_safety/)
