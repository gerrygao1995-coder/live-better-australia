Professional care, rest, safer work and connection can all matter. Seek support that fits your circumstances, and use emergency services for immediate danger.

## AU-0055 · Save crisis support before you need it

For immediate danger, call 000. For distress or a personal crisis, Lifeline provides confidential support 24/7 by phone on 13 11 14, text on 0477 13 11 14 or online chat; save the option you are most likely to use.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Lifeline support costs A$0; connection required. |
| Potential value | Makes human support easier to reach. |
| Applies to | People in Australia, including those worried about someone else. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Lifeline: Crisis Support](https://www.lifeline.org.au/get-help/national-services/lifeline-crisis-support)

## AU-0056 · Help someone connect with crisis support

If someone tells you they are struggling, listen without judgement and offer to stay with them or remain connected while they seek help. Use Lifeline for advice about supporting another person, and call 000 when their life is in immediate danger.

| Potential value | Provides company during the next step towards help. |
| Applies to | Friends and family; keep your own safety in mind. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Lifeline: Crisis Support](https://www.lifeline.org.au/get-help/national-services/lifeline-crisis-support)

## AU-0057 · Find a free mental health starting point

Check the location and opening hours of a Medicare Mental Health Centre. Centres offer free support without a referral, appointment or Mental Health Treatment Plan; they can help connect you with further care but are not an emergency crisis service.

## AU-0058 · Ask about a Mental Health Treatment Plan

Book an appropriate GP appointment to discuss your concerns and whether a treatment plan and referral are suitable. Before booking referred therapy, confirm the full fee, Medicare rebate, gap, waiting time and referral requirements; a rebate does not mean every session is free.

## AU-0059 · Protect a consistent sleep opportunity

Try a regular wake time, comfortable sleep environment and a wind-down routine that suits your household. Notice whether late caffeine, alcohol or stimulation affect sleep, and seek assessment when difficulties persist or daytime sleepiness disrupts life rather than continually buying sleep products.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Many changes cost A$0; care fees vary. |
| Potential value | Supports rest and identifies problems needing treatment. |
| Applies to | Adults; shift work and caring roles need flexibility. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Healthdirect: insomnia](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/insomnia)

<a id="au-0060"></a>

## AU-0060 · Ask about treatment for persistent insomnia

Tell your GP how long poor sleep has lasted and how it affects your day. Ask whether cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia or assessment of an underlying cause is appropriate, and check before adding sedating medicines or supplements to an existing routine.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Treatment costs and availability vary in A$. |
| Potential value | Broadens options beyond repeated self-medication. |
| Applies to | Adults with persistent sleep difficulty; never change prescribed medicines alone. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Healthdirect: insomnia](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/insomnia)

## AU-0061 · Build one reliable social connection

Choose a recurring activity that matches your interests and access needs, such as a local group, shared walk or regular phone call. Invite one person at a specific time and start small; persistent distress about loneliness is also worth discussing with a clinician.

## AU-0062 · Address harmful work conditions directly

Keep factual notes on repeated excessive demands, bullying, poor support or unclear responsibilities. Use a safe workplace reporting route and ask about changes to workload or work design; seek local regulator guidance because Safe Work Australia provides information but is not the enforcement body.

## AU-0063 · Plan alcohol reduction with appropriate support

Discuss a realistic change with your GP and identify the situations in which you most often drink. If you may be dependent, get medical advice before abruptly stopping because withdrawal can be dangerous; ask about treatment and community support that fit your circumstances.

| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0064 · Seek help when anxiety restricts daily life

Record the situations anxiety is making hard, such as travel, sleep, study or work, and discuss them with a GP or mental health professional. Ask about evidence-based treatment options and a manageable first step; avoid diagnosing yourself from an online checklist alone.

| Applies to | People with persistent or disruptive anxiety. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0065 · Describe low mood in concrete terms

Tell a clinician about changes in mood, sleep, appetite, interest and everyday functioning rather than waiting until you can explain the cause. Ask for a care plan and a review point; if there is immediate danger of self-harm, call 000.

## AU-0066 · Give grief room without a fixed timetable

Tell trusted people what help would be useful, such as company, practical tasks or time to talk. Seek a GP, counsellor or bereavement service if grief becomes overwhelming or daily functioning remains difficult; there is no single timetable that everyone must follow.

| Applies to | People experiencing bereavement or other significant loss. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |

## AU-0067 · Get support for gambling harm early

If gambling is affecting money, relationships or control over your time, contact a gambling help service or GP. Ask about counselling and practical barriers to access, and involve a trusted person if safe; do not try to recover losses by increasing stakes.

| Potential value | Interrupts a cycle of harm and concealment. |
| Applies to | People gambling and affected family members. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Healthdirect: gambling addiction](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/gambling-addiction)

## AU-0068 · Investigate fatigue that does not settle

Note when tiredness started, associated symptoms, sleep and its effect on ordinary tasks, then see your GP if it persists or is concerning. Avoid assuming the answer is an expensive supplement or pushing through unsafe drowsiness; the right response depends on the cause.

| Cost / effort | A$0 notes; assessment fees may apply. |
| Potential value | Supports assessment of treatable or manageable causes. |
| Applies to | People with ongoing fatigue; urgent symptoms need urgent care. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Healthdirect: fatigue](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/fatigue)

## AU-0069 · Use culturally safe crisis support when it fits

Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people can call 13YARN on 13 92 76 for a confidential yarn with an Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander crisis supporter. Keep the number with other support contacts; immediate danger still requires 000.

## AU-0072 · Plan one manageable activity during a difficult week

Choose one small activity you usually value, such as a short walk, meal or conversation, and decide when it could happen. Reduce the task if it feels too much and use it alongside, rather than instead of, needed professional care.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Often A$0; energy and support needs vary. |
| Potential value | Adds achievable structure without requiring a complete routine overhaul. |
| Applies to | People experiencing low mood; tailor to capacity. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Healthdirect: depression](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/depression)
