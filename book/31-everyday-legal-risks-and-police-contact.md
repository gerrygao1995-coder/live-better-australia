Legal questions depend on the jurisdiction and situation. Use calm preparation, accurate records and timely advice; a general internet rule cannot determine every police power, deadline or obligation.

## AU-0541 · Clarify whether you are free to leave

If police approach you, calmly ask whether you are under arrest or free to leave and what information they require. Legal Aid NSW distinguishes voluntary questioning from arrest and notes circumstances where identification must be provided; other jurisdictions have their own rules. Seek local advice rather than assuming every request is either compulsory or optional.

## AU-0542 · Ask for legal advice before a police interview

If asked to take part in an interview about an alleged offence, ask to contact a lawyer and explain any interpreter or support needs. Legal Aid NSW advises obtaining legal advice when unsure about answering, and describes exceptions and special-caution rules. Do not import a blanket 'say nothing in every circumstance' rule across Australia.

## AU-0543 · Record concerns without physically resisting

If concerned about a police interaction, remain calm, ask for identifying details when appropriate and seek advice about the correct complaint route afterwards. Legal Aid NSW warns that resisting arrest can itself be an offence. Record what you remember as soon as practicable, preserving evidence lawfully rather than obstructing an officer or escalating a confrontation.

## AU-0544 · Keep an emergency legal contact

Save the details of an appropriate local legal-assistance service and a trusted person who could help organise documents or appointments. Check whom the service covers and when it operates, particularly for young people or specialist needs. Store the information somewhere accessible if your usual phone is unavailable without assuming a saved number guarantees immediate representation.

## AU-0545 · Read the document before reacting to its label

When a formal letter, notice or court document arrives, identify who issued it, the case or reference number, the action requested and the response date. Verify suspicious contact through an independently found official channel, then obtain advice promptly. Keep the envelope and full document so the adviser can understand how and when it was received.

## AU-0546 · Do not let a disputed fine sit unopened

Read the notice and identify the issuing authority, payment or review options and their stated deadlines. If you disagree or cannot pay, contact the official service or an appropriate adviser promptly and keep the reference number. Check what effect, if any, a review request has before assuming it pauses another deadline.

## AU-0547 · Treat a court date as a fixed commitment

Put the date, time, location or remote link in your calendar and read all attendance instructions. Ask the registry or your adviser about access needs, interpreters, required documents and what to do if an unavoidable problem arises. Do not assume an informal request changes the date until the relevant authority confirms it.

## AU-0548 · Make a factual incident timeline

Record dates, places, people present, exact messages and what you personally observed, separating that from what someone later told you. Preserve original documents and note where they came from without editing away inconvenient details. Give the timeline privately to your adviser and ask before circulating allegations or identifying material more widely.

## AU-0549 · Check recording rules before pressing record

If considering recording a conversation, ask about the law applying to the location, participants and intended use before doing it. Recording, possessing, sharing and publishing can raise different issues, so permission for one purpose should not be assumed to cover every other purpose. A contemporaneous written note may be a useful alternative to discuss with your adviser.

## AU-0550 · Read a guarantee as your own commitment

Before guaranteeing another person's borrowing or contract, obtain independent advice on the document and the circumstances that could make you pay. Ask for the maximum exposure, duration, security and exit conditions to be explained, and consider whether you could manage that outcome. Do not sign simply because the borrower or salesperson calls it a formality.

## AU-0551 · Use the right statutory declaration

Ask the requesting organisation whether it needs a Commonwealth or state or territory declaration, then follow that form's process. Commonwealth declarations can be made through specified witnessed or digital pathways, and deliberately false statements can be an offence. Read the completed statement carefully and correct uncertainty rather than signing someone else's unsupported wording.

## AU-0552 · Ask what a witness is certifying

Before witnessing or asking someone to witness a document, identify the exact form and its instructions. A signature, certified copy and sworn or declared statement can involve different requirements, so do not assume any adult can perform every role. Give the witness time to read the instructions and leave unsigned sections untouched until the required process is clear.

## AU-0553 · Keep original evidence intact

Store original messages, photographs and documents securely and make a separate working copy for notes. Keep dates and surrounding context rather than saving only a cropped excerpt that could be misunderstood. Ask your adviser how to provide material, especially where it contains another person's sensitive information or may be subject to confidentiality obligations.

## AU-0554 · Get advice before making public allegations

If a complaint is serious, send it through an appropriate official or professional channel with supporting facts before posting accusations publicly. Distinguish your direct observations from inference, and ask a lawyer about risks if identifying a person or business. Anger can be understandable while a permanent public post still creates additional problems to resolve.

## AU-0555 · Clarify every condition after a legal decision

After receiving an order, undertaking or other formal condition, ask your lawyer or the relevant authority to explain what you must do, avoid and calendar. Put the precise requirements in a private checklist without paraphrasing away exceptions or dates. If circumstances change, seek advice about a lawful change instead of relying on an informal side arrangement.

## AU-0556 · Ask about an interpreter early

If you cannot comfortably understand a legal conversation or document, tell the service and request an appropriate interpreter or accessible format. Avoid agreeing simply to end an uncomfortable conversation, and ask how translated documents or support will be arranged. Keep a list of terms you need explained so the advice addresses your actual understanding.

## AU-0557 · Define the work you are paying a lawyer to do

Ask for the scope, fee basis, expected next step and who will handle the matter before authorising substantial work. Check whether the estimate includes disbursements and what happens if the case becomes more complex. Keep your questions organised and request an update when circumstances change instead of assuming the original estimate covers everything.

## AU-0558 · Keep advice private and check before sharing

Store legal correspondence where only authorised people can access it, and ask your lawyer who may safely receive copies. Do not assume forwarding advice to a group chat, colleague or online forum has no consequences. Share only what is needed through the agreed channel, particularly when the matter involves children, health or another person's confidential information.
