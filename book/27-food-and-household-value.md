## AU-0469 · Know which food date is a safety limit

Read whether the label says use-by or best-before and follow its storage instructions. Do not eat food after its use-by date; best-before relates to quality, but damaged packaging, unsafe storage or spoilage can still make a product unsuitable.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | A quick label check before buying or preparing food. |
| Potential value | Reduces confusion that can cause waste or unsafe choices. |
| Applies to | People handling packaged food; higher-risk individuals need extra care. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [FSANZ: Use-by and best-before dates](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/consumer/labelling/dates)

## AU-0470 · Keep a visible use-first shelf

Place food that needs using soon where you will see it, while keeping raw and ready-to-eat items safely separated. Label opened packages when the instructions specify a time after opening, and check that fridge placement follows each product's storage requirements.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | A small reorganisation and reusable labels. |
| Potential value | Makes existing food easier to use in time. |
| Applies to | Households sharing a fridge or losing track of opened food. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [FSANZ: Use-by and best-before dates](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/consumer/labelling/dates)

## AU-0471 · Cool planned leftovers promptly

When cooking food for later, follow food-safety guidance for cooling and refrigerating it rather than leaving a large pot out for hours. Use manageable containers, label the date and choose a quantity your household can safely store and consume.

| Potential value | Makes batch cooking more practical without sacrificing food safety. |
| Applies to | People preparing meals ahead; individual risk factors may need stricter advice. |

| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0472 · Separate raw ingredients from ready-to-eat food

Use separate clean utensils or thoroughly clean them between handling raw meat and food that will be eaten without cooking. Store ingredients to prevent drips or contact, and include clean-up time in the meal plan rather than rushing this step.

## AU-0473 · Read allergen information on each purchase

Check the current ingredient and allergen statements even for a familiar product, and ask for information when buying unpackaged food. Follow the advice in your own allergy management plan; a similar brand, old label or assumption about a recipe is not enough.

## AU-0474 · Check cans before stocking the pantry

Inspect cans for damage and follow the storage advice on the label. Rotate your stock so older suitable items are used first, and avoid buying a large discount quantity if the packaging or storage conditions raise questions you cannot resolve.

## AU-0480 · Fix a persistent leak through the right person

Record a visible leak and arrange the appropriate owner, water provider or licensed plumber to assess it. Keep the report and repair record, and check the next bill or meter information where available; do not attempt plumbing work that requires a licence.

## AU-0483 · Check second-hand products for recalls

Before accepting or buying an item, identify its make and model and check the ACCC Product Safety information. Look for missing components, instructions or signs of damage, and be especially cautious where an unknown history could affect safety, such as infant equipment.

| Potential value | Adds a safety check to the appeal of a lower purchase price. |
| Applies to | Buyers, borrowers and recipients of second-hand household goods. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [ACCC Product Safety](https://www.productsafety.gov.au/)
