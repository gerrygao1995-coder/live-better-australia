## AU-0523 · Separate the divorce decision from property deadlines

Ask your adviser which matters need attention now and which process deals with each one. Under the federal family-law framework, a final divorce starts an ordinary twelve-month limit for financial or property applications, with permission needed outside that period; do not assume the divorce itself settles the assets or care arrangements.

## AU-0524 · Prepare an accurate financial picture

List assets, debts, income sources and relevant records, including items you are uncertain about, and give the information to your adviser. Court disclosure obligations are continuing and an undertaking must not be false or misleading. Ask what must be disclosed and updated rather than choosing only the documents that support your preferred outcome.

## AU-0525 · Ask how an agreement becomes binding

When agreement is reached, ask a family lawyer how best to record it and whether further steps are required. Parties can seek consent orders for agreed parenting, financial or property matters, subject to the Court's requirements. Do not assume a signed email or spreadsheet has the same effect, or copy another family's orders without advice.

## AU-0526 · Check child support through the official process

Use Services Australia's child-support information to establish whether an assessment is available and what information is needed. Eligibility includes being a legal parent or eligible non-parent carer and meeting residence rules. Ask how income and care information will be used before relying on an informal estimate, and obtain advice before signing a separate agreement.

## AU-0527 · Update care information when arrangements change

Keep an accurate calendar of actual care and tell Services Australia when relevant shared-care arrangements change. Changes can affect child support and payments including Family Tax Benefit or Child Care Subsidy. Check the required reporting process and timing for each payment rather than assuming an update to one record automatically updates everything.

## AU-0528 · Report separation without guessing your payment status

Tell Services Australia about separation through the pathway for your payment and ask what other claims or information are needed. Living under the same roof can require additional assessment; if obtaining an ex-partner's information raises safety concerns, tell the agency. Do not assume a relationship label alone determines the payment rate.

## AU-0529 · Get advice before a child's travel or relocation

Before booking overseas travel or moving a child, check existing orders, proceedings and the required consent or court process with a family-law professional. Rules can apply to taking a child abroad and to keeping them away beyond authorised dates or places. Give your adviser the itinerary and current documents before paying non-refundable costs.

## AU-0530 · Include super in the property discussion

Identify each super interest and ask your adviser how it can be valued and addressed in a settlement. Superannuation splitting has formal requirements, including procedural fairness for the fund trustee; it is not achieved merely by changing a beneficiary nomination. Use the appropriate court and fund process rather than promising an informal transfer.

## AU-0531 · Make an interim essentials budget

List the coming month's housing, food, transport, medicines and child-related costs, separating confirmed money from hoped-for payments. Take the shortfall and urgent dates to an adviser or support service before making major asset decisions. An interim plan is a way to keep essentials visible, not a final agreement about property rights.

## AU-0532 · Choose a safe communication channel

If direct contact is safe and appropriate, agree one channel for practical arrangements and keep messages brief and factual. Where there are threats, monitoring or orders restricting contact, ask a lawyer or specialist support worker how to communicate safely instead. Preserve relevant messages lawfully and avoid involving children as messengers between adults.

## AU-0533 · Prepare a first-advice brief

Write a one-page timeline, list the people involved, identify any court dates and gather the main documents you can lawfully access. Put your three most urgent questions at the top and distinguish known facts from estimates or assumptions. Tell the service about safety concerns and access needs when booking rather than waiting for the appointment.

## AU-0534 · Inventory belongings without escalating the conflict

Make a factual list of significant belongings, documents and items needed for daily life, using existing records where available. Discuss a safe collection or storage arrangement through appropriate channels and get advice before taking disputed property. Keep sentimental and child-related needs visible alongside resale value rather than turning every item into a confrontation.

## AU-0535 · Check shared commitments one by one

List joint housing, utilities, loans, insurance and other contracts, then ask each provider and your adviser what changes are possible and what authority is required. Do not assume moving out removes your contractual responsibilities or that a private agreement automatically changes the provider's records. Keep confirmation of every agreed change.

## AU-0536 · Protect access to your own records safely

Review how you receive personal mail and contact from advisers, banks and services, using accounts and devices you are authorised to control. Ask a specialist safety service before making changes if monitoring or retaliation is a concern. Keep necessary records securely without entering another person's account or removing documents you are not entitled to take.

## AU-0537 · Keep children's routines in the practical plan

Write down school, health appointments, regular activities and important belongings so proposed arrangements can be checked against the child's actual week. Discuss transitions and information-sharing through safe, appropriate channels, and seek professional help where agreement is difficult. ## AU-0538 · Get a second explanation before signing under pressure

If a proposal is unclear, ask your adviser to explain the practical effect, alternatives and unresolved questions in plain language. Leave enough time to read the document and obtain independent advice, especially where the other party prepared it. Feeling tired of the dispute is understandable but does not make unclear terms easier to live with.

## AU-0539 · Budget for the process as well as the outcome

Ask each professional about scope, fee structure, likely next steps and what preparation can reduce avoidable work. Keep a running total and request a cost update when the scope changes, while discussing which issues genuinely require legal determination. A budget supports decisions but should not replace urgent advice about safety or deadlines.

## AU-0540 · Review the practical arrangements after settlement

Once formal steps are completed, list what still needs implementation: transfers, provider records, document storage, payment dates and arrangements for children. Assign responsibility and verify completion through the correct channels rather than assuming the signed document performs every action. Ask your adviser before changing an obligation that no longer seems workable.
