# 26. Climate, disasters and resilient homes

Prepare for the hazards that affect your address and the people who live there. Start with reliable warnings and an achievable plan, then evaluate building improvements and insurance with the right local expertise.

## AU-0451 · Check hazards for the exact address

Use council and state information to investigate flood, bushfire and other hazards before renting, buying or planning works. Ask what the maps do and do not show, and combine that information with inspection and insurance enquiries rather than treating any one map as a guarantee.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Research time; professional advice may be needed. |
| Potential value | Brings location risks into a major housing decision. |
| Applies to | Prospective occupants and owners; local mapping coverage varies. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Moneysmart: Prepare for a natural disaster](https://moneysmart.gov.au/dealing-with-natural-disasters/how-to-prepare-for-a-natural-disaster)

## AU-0452 · Save the official warning channels

Bookmark Bureau of Meteorology warnings and your state or territory emergency service. Choose the locations relevant to home, work and family, and work out how you will receive updates during an outage; an old screenshot or social-media post can miss a changed warning.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | A few minutes to set up reliable information sources. |
| Potential value | Makes current warnings easier to find when conditions deteriorate. |
| Applies to | Households exposed to severe weather and other local hazards. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [Bureau of Meteorology: Warnings and alerts](https://www.bom.gov.au/weather-and-climate/warnings-and-alerts)

## AU-0453 · Write down when you would leave

Use your local emergency service's planning tools to decide safe triggers, destinations and transport before a hazard arrives. Discuss the plan with everyone involved and account for disability, children, pets and anyone who may be away when action is needed.

## AU-0456 · Check flood wording in the policy

Read flood, storm and water-damage definitions alongside exclusions and excesses in the actual policy. Ask the insurer about anything unclear and obtain the answer in writing before switching; similar everyday words can refer to different insured events.

| Applies to | Home owners and renters with relevant insurance. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0457 · Review rebuilding cost after major changes

Review the sum insured after renovations or other changes that affect replacement cost. Use the insurer's guidance and ask about demolition, professional fees, temporary accommodation and rebuilding conditions, rather than using the home's market sale price as a substitute for a rebuilding estimate.

## AU-0458 · Photograph possessions before an emergency

Take a room-by-room record and save receipts or serial numbers for important items securely. Keep a copy away from the property, and update it after major purchases so a future claim does not depend entirely on remembering what was lost.

## AU-0459 · Use safe, reversible draught control

Identify obvious gaps around doors and windows and consider suitable removable draught stoppers. Check rental permission requirements before making changes, and preserve required ventilation, especially around gas appliances; ask a professional when unsure which openings should remain clear.

| Cost / effort | Low-cost materials where appropriate and permitted. |
| Potential value | May improve comfort without a major building project. |
| Applies to | Renters and owners; building and appliance requirements still apply. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0461 · Manage sun through existing window coverings

Observe which rooms gain sun at the hottest times and use suitable blinds, curtains or shading accordingly. Keep safe ventilation and access in mind, and check permissions for permanent external changes; the useful solution depends on orientation and local climate.

## AU-0463 · Ask what a battery can do in a blackout

Ask the installer which circuits, loads and duration a proposed battery can support, and whether additional backup equipment is required. Compare this with the specific needs you have identified; buying storage does not automatically mean every appliance will run during an outage.

If someone uses medical equipment at home, ask the treating team and energy retailer about applicable life-support registration and outage planning. Keep backup arrangements and emergency contacts current; registration should not be treated as a promise that supply can never fail.

## AU-0465 · Report weather damage through the rental process

Photograph the issue safely and notify the agent or landlord promptly in writing. Use your jurisdiction's urgent-repair and safety procedures, and seek local tenancy advice if the home becomes unsafe or uninhabitable rather than assuming ordinary lease obligations remain unchanged.

| Cost / effort | Documentation and contact time; avoid unsafe inspection work. |
| Potential value | Creates a clear record and directs urgent problems to the right process. |
| Applies to | Renters affected by leaks, storms, flooding or other damage. |
| Basis | Practical suggestion |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

## AU-0466 · Keep outdoor hazards on a seasonal checklist

Use your local emergency service's advice to identify loose items, drainage and vegetation issues that need attention before severe weather. Arrange competent help for heights, electrical hazards or work beyond your ability, and obtain permission where responsibility belongs to an owner or strata body.

| Potential value | Can reduce avoidable hazards around the property. |

## AU-0467 · Do not use floodwater as a shortcut

Check current road closures and choose another route when flooding affects travel. Never drive into floodwater or past a closure; the depth, road surface and current may be impossible to judge, and a vehicle does not make the route safe.

| At a glance | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Cost / effort | Extra time or postponing the trip. |
| Potential value | Avoids turning a disrupted journey into a life-threatening emergency. |
| Applies to | All road users in flood-affected areas. |
| Basis | Official / professional guidance |
| Editorial review | 2026-10-09 |

**Read the source:** [NSW State Emergency Service](https://www.ses.nsw.gov.au/)
