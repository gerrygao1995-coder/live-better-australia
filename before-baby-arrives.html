Before your baby arrives: the Australian admin checklist

Eight practical jobs to organise leave, payments and care costs now, and prepare the paperwork you will finish after birth.

Before you start: For households expecting a baby in Australia. Payment eligibility, workplace entitlements and insurance cover depend on your circumstances; registration processes differ by state and territory. This checklist focuses on administration, costs and timing.

0 of 8 ticked off

Ticks stay in this browser where storage is available. No account or personal details needed.

  1. Ask the maternity service about booking, referrals and out-of-pocket costs for appointments, tests, birth and follow-up. If using private cover, confirm waiting periods, exclusions and how and when to add your newborn. Save the answers before committing to a care pathway.

    Better Health Channel: Paying for pregnancy, birth and newborn care ↗

    In the full guide: AU-0344 · AU-0345

  2. Check employer-funded pay and eligibility for unpaid parental leave separately. Under national Fair Work rules, eligible employees generally give 10 weeks’ written notice; continuous leave dates must be confirmed at least four weeks before starting. If the required notice is impossible, notify the employer as soon as possible.

    Fair Work Ombudsman: Applying for parental leave ↗

    In the full guide: AU-0201 · AU-0177

  3. Check the work, income, residence and caring rules, including each parent's eligibility and any sharing rules. You can submit a claim up to three months before the expected birth. Set up Centrelink through myGov, gather the required details and remember that payment cannot start before birth and proof is required.

    Services Australia: Who can get Parental Leave Pay ↗ Services Australia: How to claim Parental Leave Pay ↗

    In the full guide: AU-0201

  4. Map confirmed take-home income against housing, bills, baby essentials and care costs. Keep unapproved payments separate from money you can rely on, and check whether other family payments may apply. Agree who pays shared bills and which purchases can wait.

    Moneysmart: Having a baby ↗

    In the full guide: AU-0199 · AU-0201 · AU-0209

  5. Ask services about vacancies, waitlists, session fees and your estimated bill after Child Care Subsidy. The 3 Day Guarantee applies to CCS-eligible families; it guarantees subsidised hours, not a place or free care. Plan the CCS claim before care starts, and confirm the provider's enrolment details through Centrelink.

    Department of Education: 3 Day Guarantee ↗ Services Australia: Who can get Child Care Subsidy ↗ Services Australia: How to claim Child Care Subsidy ↗

    In the full guide: AU-0202 · AU-0209

  6. Check your Medicare eligibility and online access now. If still on your parents' card, transfer to your own before enrolling your baby. After birth, use the signed Newborn Child Declaration as proof and enrol through the applicable family-payment claim or Medicare process. Babies born overseas, adopted or born through surrogacy have separate instructions.

    Services Australia: Enrolling your baby in Medicare ↗

    In the full guide: AU-0578 · AU-0345

  7. Bookmark your state or territory birth-registration process: registration is free, while a certificate may cost extra. Separately, finish any pre-birth Centrelink claim with the baby's details and proof of birth. For a pre-birth Parental Leave Pay claim, Services Australia says to provide these within 56 days of the child entering your care.

    Services Australia: Register the birth of your baby ↗ Services Australia: Parental Leave Pay claiming timeframes ↗

    In the full guide: AU-0201

  8. Agree who will manage bills, paperwork, meals, transport and any older children's pickups during the first weeks. Keep deadlines and confirmations in one shared plan, and check that helpers have the necessary collection permissions. Share only the information they need; keep passwords private.

    In the full guide: AU-0357 · AU-0207 · AU-0177

Start with the next deadline, then work through the rest at your own pace. Keep claim receipts and written confirmations; revisit the plan if the birth date, leave or care arrangements change.
