Renting your first place? Read this before you sign

Use this checklist at the inspection and before paying or signing. Save the move-in steps for when you collect the keys.

Before you start: For people renting in Australia. Tenancy rules are state and territory based; boarding, rooming, student accommodation and other arrangements may use different rules. The NSW example below is labelled and is not a national deadline.

0 of 8 ticked off

Ticks stay in this browser where storage is available. No account or personal details needed.

  1. Find your state or territory tenancy authority. Check which rules cover your accommodation type.

    myGov — Signing a rental lease and links for every state and territory ↗

    In the full guide: AU-0093 · AU-0099

  2. Write two totals: the cash needed to move in, and the ongoing cost of living there. Include bond, permitted advance rent, moving, utilities, internet and transport. Check whether your budget still works after a quieter work week or an unexpected bill.

    In the full guide: AU-0091 · AU-0098

  3. Inspect the home. Independently verify the provider before sending money or identity documents.

    myGov — Checks before signing a rental lease ↗

    In the full guide: AU-0091 · AU-0092

  4. Check the names, address, rent, start date, lease length, payment method and extra conditions. Ask what happens if you need to leave early, and have unclear terms checked by a local tenancy service. Keep the agreement and any written repair promises.

    Moneysmart — Understanding rental leases ↗

    In the full guide: AU-0091 · AU-0101

  5. For NSW, check Rental Bonds Online and keep your payment record. Elsewhere, use the authority you found in step 1. Agree and record each housemate's contribution.

    NSW Government — Rental Bonds Online for tenants (NSW only) ↗

    In the full guide: AU-0092 · AU-0093 · AU-0094

  6. Agree on rent, bills, bond contributions and what happens when someone leaves. Check whose names are on the lease and obtain local advice about each person's responsibilities. A household spreadsheet or private promise does not by itself change the rental agreement.

    NSW Government — Sharing a rental property (NSW example) ↗

    In the full guide: AU-0097

  7. Photograph existing damage before unpacking, complete the condition report and keep a copy plus proof of return. Check your local deadline: in NSW, the tenant returns a completed copy within seven days of moving in. Use your own jurisdiction's rule elsewhere.

    NSW Government — Rental property condition reports (NSW only) ↗

    In the full guide: AU-0092

  8. Ask whether electricity is supplied through an embedded network, who bills you and what tariffs and fees apply. Check your actual ability to choose another retailer before relying on a comparison quote. Add the confirmed arrangement to your housing budget.

    Australian Energy Regulator — Embedded network customers ↗

    In the full guide: AU-0100

Keep the lease, receipts, photos and correspondence together. Before signing an unclear term or paying an unfamiliar charge, check the current rule with your state or territory tenancy authority or advice service.
