Lost your job in Australia? Start here

Start with deadlines and the next fortnight's essentials. You can work through the rest with help.

Before you start: For employees in Australia. Federal workplace rules below apply to the national system; many state public-sector and council roles, and some WA employers, use state systems. Payment eligibility and claim options depend on your circumstances.

Ticks stay in this browser where storage is available. No account or personal details needed.

  1. If you may challenge the dismissal, contact the Fair Work Commission, your union or a legal service now. National unfair-dismissal applications generally have a 21-day deadline after dismissal takes effect. Eligibility conditions apply; ask about other claims too, rather than ruling yourself out.

    Fair Work Ombudsman — Unfair dismissal ↗ Fair Work Ombudsman — Which workplace system applies ↗

    In the full guide: AU-0166 · AU-0163 · AU-0180

  2. Save your contract, payslips, rosters, dismissal letter and a dated account of what happened. Keep records you can lawfully retain somewhere you control. Ask for missing employment records; keep employer-confidential files out of your personal archive.

    Fair Work Ombudsman — Record-keeping and pay slips ↗

    In the full guide: AU-0164 · AU-0179

  3. Check wages owing and any accrued annual leave. Ask whether notice pay, redundancy pay or long service leave applies; these are not universal entitlements. Confirm the payment deadline under your award, agreement and applicable law instead of assuming one final-pay deadline covers everyone.

    Fair Work Ombudsman — Final pay ↗

    In the full guide: AU-0179 · AU-0163

  4. Check which payment fits your age and circumstances. JobSeeker has income, assets and residence rules; waiting periods can apply, including after leave or redundancy payouts. Gather requested evidence and keep claim references. Budget around confirmed money, not an unconfirmed approval or payment date.

    Services Australia — JobSeeker Payment ↗ Services Australia — When you will get your first JobSeeker Payment ↗

    In the full guide: AU-0145

  5. List available cash, confirmed income, rent or mortgage, food, medicines, utilities and necessary transport. Mark the next due dates. Identify the first shortfall so you can ask for specific help before making new commitments.

    In the full guide: AU-0707 · AU-0152

  6. Contact providers about affordable payment arrangements and keep written records. For free, confidential financial counselling, call the National Debt Helpline on 1800 007 007. Bring urgent notices and the essentials plan; tell the counsellor which payment is due first.

    Moneysmart — Financial counselling ↗

    In the full guide: AU-0148 · AU-0153

  7. Update one useful CV example, contact a reference or compare a few suitable roles. If retraining would help, check your state's Free TAFE options first: courses, eligibility and places are limited. Confirm materials, placement and living costs before enrolling.

    Department of Employment and Workplace Relations — Free TAFE ↗

    In the full guide: AU-0198 · AU-0181 · AU-0191

If you only do one thing today, check any dismissal deadline. These are starting steps, not an assessment of your legal claim or payment eligibility. Open the current official source before acting.
